# Nevada — Shadow Creek: what was published, and the verdict behind each sentence

The sentence-by-sentence audit trail for `priv/seed_data/golf/nevada.json`,
the flagship Ruling 9 state of this wave: Golf Digest labels the ranked
course "Public," and the operator's own terms show it is not bookable by a
member of the public at all.

**This file is committed on purpose.** The research artifacts live under
`.superpowers/`, which `.gitignore` excludes. A year from now this file is
the only thing that can answer "where did that sentence come from."

Sources, in order of authority:

1. `.superpowers/sdd/2026-09-05-golf-courses/research/nevada-verdicts.md` —
   the independent verifier's adjudication. **The authority.** Tally: **24
   confirmed · 11 refuted · 6 uncertain (41 adjudicated claims)** — the most
   refutations of any state in this wave.
2. `.superpowers/sdd/2026-09-05-golf-courses/research/nevada-finder.md` — the
   finder. Context only. It carries all eleven refuted claims, including
   three fabricated evidence quotes; where the two disagree, the verdicts
   file wins, always.

**The governing rule:** a verdict vouches only for what its own text
restates, not for every clause of the sentence the finder wrote around it.

**The refuted-with-correction rule:** where a verdict is `refuted` but
supplies a correction naming what may still be published, that correction's
text ships **verbatim**, and nothing else from the item does.

**10 places, 9 entries, 1 guide, 5 sections, 5 FAQ answers, 0 photos.**

## 0. Ruling 9 — the contradiction this guide exists to state

Golf Digest's Nevada intro, verbatim (verdict §1.4): "public golfers in
Nevada are lucky. Thirteen of the top 20 courses in Nevada ranked below are
accessible to the public (though many with high green fees)." Golf Digest's
own words hedge only on **price**, never on **eligibility**. The brief's
hypothesis that "Public" might mean "not member-owned" is explicitly refuted
by Golf Digest's own text and is never suggested in the published guide.

What actually governs Shadow Creek, per the operator (verdict §3.1, §3.3,
§3.4): a qualifying MGM Resorts stay is required; Monday–Thursday a hotel
guest may call and request a time; Friday and Saturday are for comped
players with a casino host and a gambling credit line; and the general
manager's own words include "Some Sundays we open up."

Per the brief's Ruling 9, this contradiction is stated **in the intro**
(`guide.intro`, sentences 3–6) **and in the FAQ** (`"Is Shadow Creek really
a public course?"`), and the second course, TPC Las Vegas, is presented in
the intro's closing sentence and in a full FAQ entry as the practical round
for an ordinary traveller — not buried at the end of the guide.

## 3. The eleven refuted claims — what was not published, and why

Every refutation in `nevada-verdicts.md`, named:

**R1 (§1.3) — the finder's quoted Golf Digest sentence.** "Shadow Creek is
Nevada's No.1 course (Golf Digest, 2025/26) and ranks as USA's 24th best
course, and 3rd best public access course" does not appear on
`golfdigest.com/courses/nv/shadow-creek`. Golf Digest prints terse rank
chips, not that sentence — third-party prose the finder attributed to the
wrong page. **Correction published instead, verbatim**, in the intro and
Shadow Creek section.

**R2 (§1.4) — "Public" means "not member-owned."** The brief's own
hypothesis, refuted by Golf Digest's own text ("accessible to the public").
Never published in either direction; instead, Ruling 9's contradiction is
stated directly (§0 above).

**R3 (§3.2) — "mandatory caddie and private limo transfer from the guest's
MGM hotel lobby."** golf.com's actual words are "a private limo ride to the
golf course, caddie and locker" — no "mandatory," no "from the guest's MGM
hotel lobby." Worse, The Golf News Net (Apr 2026) says the caddie is billed
separately, contradicting "included" outright. **Not published**; replaced
by the correction stating the limo is included and the caddie is disputed.

**R4 (§4.2) — FABRICATED QUOTE: "Green Fee: $1,250 per round (as of December
2024)."** The verifier grepped the raw HTML of golf.com's article twice and
confirmed this labelled sentence does not exist anywhere in it. **Never
published, in any form.** Replaced with "golf.com reported the green fee at
$1,250 a round on December 6, 2024," used in the "What does it cost"
FAQ answer and nowhere else.

**R5 (§5.1) — FABRICATED QUOTE: "This premier Strip resort features
four-star luxury accommodations"** (MGM Grand). Not on
underpar.com/courses/shadow-creek-mgm-grand-stay-play; the actual text
never says "four-star" or "Strip resort." **Never published.** MGM Grand's
place summary and section prose instead say only that it is an MGM Resorts
International property on the Strip at its Marriott-confirmed address.

**R6 (§5.2) — FABRICATED QUOTE: "3,933 guest rooms and suites, this AAA
5-Diamond Award winning resort hotel, spa, entertainment centre and
casino"** (Bellagio). Not on eaglegolftours.com/resort/the-bellagio; that
page carries no room count and no award language at all. This is also an
award claim, which the sourcing rule forbids regardless of citation quality.
**Never published, in any form, including the room count and the AAA
claim.** Bellagio's summary says only where it sits on the Strip.

**R7 (§5.3) — the Le Cirque quote, truncated.** The finder's version, "The
French restaurant Le Cirque is one of the favourite fine dining locations,"
drops the source's own "a renowned pre-theatre dinner location" clause. The
fact stands; the truncated wording does not. **Published instead, whole**:
the complete sentence, with the added note that Eagle Golf Tours is a tour
operator, not Bellagio, so no trading claim is made.

**R8 (§6.4) — "7,106 yards" for TPC Las Vegas.** A digit transposition; the
operator's own site says 7,016. **Never published** in the wrong form;
7,016 is what appears in both the course place summary and the Day Two
section.

**R9 (§6.5) — "Las Vegas Senior Classic (1997–1999)."** Wikipedia gives
1994–2001 on the Champions Tour; not mentioned at all on tpc.com. **Wrong
years never published**; 1994–2001 is what ships.

**R10 (§8.6) — "Debuted on America's 100 Greatest at No. 8 in 1993-'94 —
the highest appearance by a new course in the ranking's history."** The
cited page, top100golfcourses.com's "10 Interesting Facts," contains no
1993-'94 debut, no No. 8, and no "highest appearance by a new course" — a
pure superlative accretion from a search snippet. **Never published, in any
form.**

**R11 (§2.1) — "Shadow Creek Golf Course is in Las Vegas, Nevada" / "sits
north of North Las Vegas."** Both wrong on jurisdiction: the US Census
Bureau geocoder places the course's address inside North Las Vegas city
limits, in Clark County, for every address/ZIP permutation tested. **Never
published**; the guide states North Las Vegas throughout, and the title is
built specifically to avoid this framing (§1 above).

A twelfth item is a citation correction rather than a refuted fact: §2.2
and §8.4 both replace the finder's citations (GolfPass for the county;
top100golfcourses for MGM ownership since 2000) with better authorities (the
Census geocoder; golf.com) that actually state what is claimed. Nothing
published changed as a result, only what backs it.

worth recording: the Nevada *guide* page carries no edition string of its

**U4 (§6.6) — Shriners Hospitals for Children Open, 2000–2006.** Not on
tpc.com, not on the TPC Las Vegas Wikipedia article. No confirming source.
**Omitted entirely.**

**U5 (§8.2) — year Shadow Creek opened.** golf.com and top100golfcourses say
1989; Golf Digest's own course profile and Wikipedia's infobox say 1990.
**Published as the hedge**, not a bare settled year: "opened... 1989... Golf

**U6 (§8.5 caveat) — exact CJ Cup dates and "@ Nine Bridges" styling.** Only
the month and year (October 2020) were independently confirmed. **Published

## 5. What is absent from the whole file

* **No trip duration of any kind, and no mileage of any kind.** Every
  source for Shadow Creek–to-airport/Strip distance gives a duration only
  (§7.3); the guide states the gap exists rather than converting or
  inventing a figure.
* **No vague proximity.** Bali Hai's location is named with no directional
  or "near"-type qualifier (§ Section 5 above) even though the verdict's own
  narrative prose used one uncited.
* **No price without the source's own attached date.** Both green-fee
  figures carry their source's own date: golf.com, Dec 6 2024; The Golf News
  Net, 2026.
* **No trading claim for any restaurant, hotel, or lodging property.**
  Stated explicitly in the basecamp section's prose, matching the pattern
  Oregon's F2 discipline set.
* **No property list for MGM qualification**, anywhere, including inside an
  otherwise verbatim quote.
* **No 1993-'94 debut, no "Michelin-starred," no AAA award, no room count,
  no "four-star," no fabricated fee-quote, no 7,106 yards, no 1997–1999, no
  "Shadow Creek is in Las Vegas."** All eleven refuted items, and the three
  fabricated quotes specifically, are absent from every field of the JSON,
  not merely rephrased.

## 6. What the gate caught, and what it did not

Run: `MIX_TEST_PARTITION=_golf mix test test/ethos/seeds/golf_seed_data_test.exs`
— **9 tests, 0 failures, on the first run.** No allowlist entry was added;
`Ethos.GolfProse`'s allowlist remains `[]`.

The gate did not need to catch anything in drafting because the prose was
written against the verdicts' own "must not publish" list first — in
particular, "nearer the Strip" and "near the south end of the Strip"
(present in an early draft, describing Bali Hai, lifted unreflectively from
the verdict document's own closing narrative) were caught on a self-review
before running the suite and rewritten with no directional qualifier at
all, per the discipline `docs/golf/oregon.md` §4 records: "a rule you
avoided without testing is a rule you cannot prove you avoided" — checked
here by re-reading Ethos.GolfProse's pattern list directly (`test/support/
golf_prose.ex`) rather than trusting the phrase looked safe.

**What the gate structurally cannot catch, per its own moduledoc:** an
unsourced *containment* or *identity* claim in ordinary prose is
grammatically identical to a sourced one beside it. The defence for this
file is this trace, not the regex — for example, nothing in `GolfProse`
would have caught "Shadow Creek is in Las Vegas" (§2.1's refutation) had it
been written; only reading the verdict and choosing "North Las Vegas"
throughout does.
