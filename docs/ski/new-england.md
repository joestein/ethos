# New England ski corpus — destination tree

## Known inconsistency: Boston sits beside its counties, not inside one

Massachusetts has one region node (`united-states/massachusetts`) and, before
this task, exactly one child: `united-states/massachusetts/boston`, a `city`
sitting directly under the region with no county tier above it.

Task 3 adds Massachusetts county nodes (Berkshire, Essex, Franklin, Middlesex,
Norfolk, Worcester) as children of the region, for the ski areas that need
them. Boston is not re-parented under Suffolk County — it stays exactly where
it was, a `city` node sitting beside the counties rather than inside one.

This is deliberate, not an oversight:

- The inconsistency predates this project.
- The destination tree is variable-depth by design; not every branch needs
  the same number of tiers.
- Re-parenting Boston would move a live URL for no benefit to this corpus.

Leave it as-is. Verification tables for the New England ski content waves
belong in later tasks, not here.

## Recurring defect shapes

Three waves of adversarial verification, roughly 350 claims deep, keep
catching the same small set of defect shapes. This section exists for
whoever builds the Rockies, the Midwest, and the rest of the corpus after
this task: these are the failure modes to expect, not a checklist that
protects against new ones.

1. **An announcement reported as a completed event.** Burke Mountain's
   "sold in 2025" was a pending, conditional deal — Bear Den Partners had
   been *announced* as a buyer, not closed as one (Wave 1).
2. **Unsupported precision bolted onto a supported fact.** A year, a street
   address, or a place name the source never gives, attached to a claim
   that is otherwise true — e.g. Black Mountain's "Jackson, NH"
   headquarters detail, true but cited to a letter that never names the
   town (Wave 3). Its strongest form is a source that explicitly disclaims
   knowledge while the claim supplies the fact anyway: Whaleback's cited
   page states "it is not known when the area opened," published in the
   finder's file as "opened in mid-February" (Wave 4).
3. **A quotation altered by a scope-changing word.** A dropped "major"
   turned a true claim false; a dropped "likely" turned a source's own
   hedge into an unhedged assertion (Wave 2's Smugglers' Notch and Abenaki
   patterns).
4. **Two dated events merged into one.** An acquisition and a later,
   separate rebranding or expansion collapsed into a single date — Magic
   Mountain's 1986 Timber Ridge acquisition and its 1987-88 physical
   connection and rebrand (Wave 2).
5. **A citation that predates or postdates its own event.** Ski Magazine
   cited for a 2022 sentencing it reported before the sentencing happened
   (Wave 1's Jay Peak/Stenger claim).
6. **A claim whose cited page does not contain it.** Bretton Woods'
   "in close partnership with the Omni Mount Washington Resort & Spa"
   quote is not on brettonwoods.com anywhere the verifier checked,
   including the sitemap (Wave 3).
7. **A scope defect hiding behind correct citations.** Arrowhead Recreation
   Area: every claim in the file was accurately sourced, and the area still
   had no place in this corpus, because its lift closed in 1990 and it is
   not lift-served skiing at all (Wave 3).
8. **A fabricated quotation attributed to a named source.** Gunstock's file
   attributed "growing tensions with the five-member commission" to WMUR;
   the phrase is not in the article, checked twice against the full text —
   one occurrence in roughly 390 claims across three waves (Wave 3).
9. **A relative date resolved against the wrong anchor.** "Last month" is
   only meaningful against its source's own publication date; copied into
   prose carrying a different date anchor, it silently changes what it
   says — Ragged Mountain's "last month," in a November 4, 2025 article,
   meant October 2025, not the April 2025 a neighbouring May 2025 date
   implied (Wave 4).
10. **A citation chain that misattributes its underlying source.** Citing
    publication A "as cited in" article B, where B actually cites
    publication C — Wildcat's file credited the Union Leader "as cited in
    Wikipedia's Peak Resorts article," but that Wikipedia article cites
    Forbes, not the Union Leader (Wave 4).
11. **A last-modified date read as a publication date.** A CMS exposing
    both invites citing the wrong one — Millinocket's WinterKids grant
    page carries a `dateModified` of September 4, 2025 that the finder's
    file cited as when the page was published; the page's own `datePublished`
    is July 5, 2024, over a year earlier (Wave 5).
12. **An empty list with no stated reason is a research gap, not a finding.**
    Three empty `surroundings` lists have been challenged in this project
    and all three were wrong — Baker Mountain's and Big Rock's empty lists
    (Wave 5) each turned out to have a wind farm, a dam, a library, or a
    National Register marker sitting untouched in the same town. An empty
    list that names what was checked and what was deliberately excluded is
    a finding; one that names nothing is an unanswered question.
13. **A row that contradicts its own sibling row.** Mount Jefferson's claim
    of "nearly 30 years" of Delano-family ownership sits against its own
    file's 1978-80 sole-ownership date for the same family, which implies
    36-38 years to the 2016 sale, not nearly 30 (Wave 5).
14. **A hedge preserved but its word changed.** Keeping a hedge is not
    enough if the hedge's own word does not survive — Powderhouse Hill's
    "around 1939" for a source that repeats "about 1939" three times
    (Wave 6).
15. **A near-synonym substituted in a quotation.** Each reads as harmless
    on its own and each changes what the source actually said: Vaughan
    Woods' "donated" for a source's "bequeathed" (a will-based transfer,
    not a lifetime gift), Titcomb Mountain's "benefited from" for "thanks
    in part to," and Spruce Mountain's "added" for "upgraded" (only the
    bathrooms were new; the warming hut, snack bar, and garage already
    existed) (Wave 6).
16. **Two accounts a source itself flags as discrepant, spliced into one.**
    The inverse of quoting a self-contradicting source: NELSAP's
    Powderhouse Hill page presents a 1975 club-history-book account and a
    separate contributors'-recollection account of the tow engine's
    history that the source itself calls out as disagreeing, and a claim
    spliced them into one uncontested history. Attribute the disagreement;
    do not resolve it silently (Wave 6).
17. **A PO box or postal address treated as evidence of municipality.**
    Saddleback's own mailing address uses "Rangeley, ME 04970" because
    that is the nearest post office, but Saddleback Mountain itself sits
    in Sandy River Plantation, a separate municipality from the town of
    Rangeley — mail routing says nothing about where a building stands
    (Wave 6).

### Town rule, applied

Each area's places sit in that area's own town, not a more-famous neighbor:
Attitash's places are in Bartlett, not North Conway (which is in Conway, a
different town); Cranmore's are in Conway, where North Conway village
genuinely sits; Bretton Woods' hotel is addressed to the unincorporated
village of Bretton Woods itself, within the town of Carroll; Crotched
Mountain's are in Francestown, per Ski New Hampshire's own base-lodge
address, not Bennington, which several sources use loosely for the
mountain's western slopes; Dartmouth Skiway's are in Lyme, not the college
town of Hanover the Skiway is "descended from"; Granite Gorge's are in
Roxbury, not the Keene mailing address its own site may use.

### Links, and why so few

Only two links were made across all eleven files, both between areas built
in this same wave: Attitash to Cranmore (Attitash's founder, Phil Robertson,
carried Cranmore's wide trails, ski shop, and restaurant manager with him —
a real editorial connection, not proximity) and Attitash to Mount Snow
(sold together to Peak Resorts on the same day in 2007, for one price, in
a Wave 2 guide). Black Mountain, Attitash, and Bretton Woods all sit in or
near the Mount Washington Valley, and none of the three link to each
other — proximity alone, without a sourced editorial connection, is not a
link per this task's own instruction.
