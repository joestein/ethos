# Tropicana Field — what was published, and the verdict behind each sentence

The sentence-by-sentence audit trail for
`lib/ethos/seeds/tropicana_field_places.ex` and
`lib/ethos/seeds/tropicana_field_guide.ex`.

**This file is committed on purpose.** The research artifact lives under
`.superpowers/`, which `.gitignore` excludes. A moduledoc citing a git-ignored
path has no audit trail a year from now; the moduledocs cite this file instead.

Source: the 2026-08-30 Tampa Bay Rays research artifact
(`.superpowers/sdd/2026-08-30-mlb-ballparks/research/tampa-bay-rays.json`,
`verified: true`), adjudicated by an independent adversarial verifier.

**The governing rule:** a verdict vouches only for what its own `item` text
restates. Only `confirmed` verdicts ship — and a clause the verifier's own text
**expressly states was confirmed** is an adjudication of that clause and ships
too. A finder's refuted item never ships as written.

**8 places and 1 guide shipped** from 8 researched places. **No place was
dropped.**

## 0. The fabricated evidence, stated first

This is the artifact in which the verifier found **invented evidence quotes**.
Its own verification note:

> Two 'still_trading' evidence quotes (Salvador Dalí Museum, Museum of Fine
> Arts) were found to be fabricated — the exact phrases attributed to Wikipedia
> do not appear in those articles after full-text search.

The two phrases are *"remains operational and actively hosts exhibitions,
educational programs, and community events"* and *"active and operating"*. Both
were offered as proof that a museum was trading. **Neither exists in the article
it was attributed to.**

**What that changed:**

* **Salvador Dalí Museum — downgraded from `open` to `uncertain`.** The
  verifier: *"Once that quote is removed, the article's own most-recent dated
  content is from the 2021-2022 school year and 2023 student exhibitions —
  three-plus years stale relative to today — with no closure indication either.
  The museum's own site shows live 2026 ticketing and named current
  exhibitions, but per the contract's rule 4 a business/institution's own site
  cannot by itself establish that it is still trading … This is a real downgrade
  from the finder's 'open,' not a rubber stamp."* **No trading claim is made**,
  in the record, in the guide prose or in the FAQ, and the guide explains why in
  those terms.
* **Museum of Fine Arts — status survived, on different evidence.** *"[U]nlike
  the Dalí Museum, genuine independent evidence survives the correction: the
  article does state 'Klaudio Rodriguez has served as Executive Director and CEO
  since 2024' and separately mentions exhibitions scheduled through 2025-2026 …
  On that corrected evidence, 'open' still stands, but on a narrower basis than
  the finder claimed."* **The narrower basis is what the guide publishes** — the
  dated appointment and the scheduled exhibitions, not the invented phrase.

**Everything else in the artifact survived.** The refutations here are about
invented evidence for trading status and two overstatements, not about wrong
facts: every other figure, date and name the finder recorded was re-fetched and
matched.

## 1. Places, against the verdicts they rest on

**Addresses ship only where a verdict or the verifier's own text carries one.**
Three do: Tropicana Field's (confirmed twice, from the club and the
encyclopedia), the Chihuly Collection's (from the verifier's own correction),
and Green Bench Brewing's (*"address matched exactly"*). **Five do not** — the
Dalí Museum, the St. Pete Pier, Ferg's, Al Lang Stadium and the Museum of Fine
Arts — because the artifact's place header proposes an address for each that no
verdict restates. Each of those records says so on its face, and an FAQ answer
explains the pattern.

### tropicana-field — Tropicana Field (`stadium`)

`One Tropicana Drive, St. Petersburg, Florida 33705` · `https://www.mlb.com/rays/ballpark`

Two confirmed place verdicts, three confirmed identity verdicts and six
confirmed `history` items, all published: the March 3, 1990 opening; the current
name since 1996 under a 30-year Tropicana Products deal signed that October, and
the Florida Suncoast Dome and ThunderDome eras before it; the fixed roof, angled
to reduce interior volume and cooling costs; the 25,025 capacity dated to 2019
and the Shaw Sports Turf surface since 2017; the three architecture practices;
the Cownose Clubhouse tank at 35 feet and 10,000 US gallons; the 2008 World
Series and the four-games-to-one loss to the Phillies; the October 9, 2024
Hurricane Milton damage, the 2025 season at George M. Steinbrenner Field, the
approximately $55 million repairs and the April 6, 2026 return with its 6-4 win
over the Cubs; and the cancelled $1.2 billion Gas Plant project, the $2.3
billion Dale Mabry campus project targeting 2029 at 30,000 to 35,000 capacity,
and the "New Ballpark" link on MLB's own page.

**The superlative ships and the verifier explains why it may:** *"The 'only
active MLB stadium with a fixed… roof' sentence is genuinely in the article,
tied explicitly to Target Field's 2010 opening (when the Metrodome closed) —
this is a sourced superlative, not one that accreted in transcription."* It is
published as the encyclopedia's description, with the tie-in.

**Two source disagreements are published rather than resolved.** The repair cost
is approximately $55 million in the ballpark article and nearly $60 million in
the club article — *"an inconsistency inside Wikipedia itself, not an error
introduced by the finder"*. And the club's own floated alternative on the Gas
Plant cancellation, which the verifier adds from the article, is published
beside the Dale Mabry plan.

`status_verdict: confirmed`, and the verifier calls it *"the strongest-evidenced
'open' status in this batch"* — two independently re-fetched sources agreeing on
a specific checkable event, the April 6, 2026 return and its score.

### st-pete-pier — St. Pete Pier (`attraction`)

`address: nil` · `https://stpetepier.org/`

Two confirmed verdicts, published in full: the July 6, 2020 opening, the 26
acres, the $92 million cost, the May 2015 voter approval and the June 28, 2017
groundbreaking, and the five restaurants, playground, environmental education
center and Janet Echelman's "Bending Arc"; and the pier's own published hours.

**A caveat the verifier told the author to carry, and it is carried:** *"'Bending
Arc' is confirmed to exist at the pier via the pier's own site, though as of
that fetch the sculpture itself is separately labeled 'Temporarily Closed' —
worth carrying as a caveat on the artwork specifically, not on the pier as a
whole."* Published exactly that way, on the sculpture and not the pier.

**One verdict is `refuted` as an overstatement, and the corrected form ships.**
The finder wrote that the site has held a landmark structure since 1889; the
verifier: *"The article dates the first *centralized* pier — the Railroad Pier,
built by the Orange Belt Railway — to 1889, but also states smaller piers
existed on the waterfront going back to 1854."* Published as the verifier
corrects it: structures from at least 1854, the first large centralized pier in
1889, with the Million Dollar Pier of 1926 and the Inverted Pyramid Pier of
1973-2013 standing as given. The Mediterranean Revival style ships because a
verdict states it.

**The opening time is dropped for the duration ban**, and this is the wave's
clearest instance of the rule the dispatch describes. The confirmed hours item
gives a closing time and an opening time expressed as an interval before
sunrise. The interval is duration-shaped; the corpus's gate scans module source;
its allowlist is keyed `{file, phrase}` over a whole module and cannot be
narrowed, so pardoning it would pardon every duration-shaped string in that
file, including one added later. **The clause was dropped rather than the gate
weakened**, the closing time is published, and both the module and the guide say
in terms that an opening time exists and why it is not here. Because the ban
scans source, neither moduledoc quotes the clause it records dropping.

`status_verdict: confirmed`, on the encyclopedia's ongoing present tense plus a
city-run site current for this year.

### chihuly-collection-st-petersburg — Chihuly Collection (`museum`)

`720 Central Avenue, St. Petersburg, Florida` · `https://www.moreanartscenter.org/visit/chihuly-collection/`

Two confirmed verdicts, published as the verifier corrected them: the Morean
Arts Center's operation of the collection, its 1917 founding as the Art Club of
St. Petersburg by Margaret and Edith Tadd, Dale Chihuly's 2010 collaboration,
and the 2016 relocation; and the hours from its own site with their holiday
exceptions, which the verifier notes the finder *"correctly [did] not treat…as
evidence of current trading status"*.

**The spatial clause was dropped on the verifier's instruction, and the
reasoning is the corpus's own rule stated by someone else:**

> However, "next to the Morean Arts Center's main building" is a banned
> vague-proximity phrase under Hard Rule 2 (same class as "next door"), even
> though the Wikipedia source itself uses similar language ("next door,"
> "adjacent"). A source using vague proximity does not license republishing it
> — unlike architectural style, this rule has no sourced-exception clause.
>
> **Correction:** Drop the spatial-relationship clause. State only: "the
> collection relocated to 720 Central Avenue in 2016."

That is published verbatim in substance: the street and the year, and nothing
about what stands beside it. It is also where the address comes from.

`status_verdict: confirmed as uncertain` — the verifier re-checked the article
for present-tense operational language about the Chihuly Collection specifically
and found none. **No trading claim**, and the `could_not_establish` is
published.

## 2. The guide

### Getting there

All four `getting_there` items are confirmed and all four ship: the free Central
Avenue Trolley between Grand Central Station and the St. Pete Pier, roughly 6:30
a.m. to 10 p.m. daily; the SunRunner bus rapid transit line and the four
districts it connects, near-verbatim from PSTA's own description; the seven
numbered lots with wheelchair-accessible parking in Lots 1 and 7 and most
accessible spaces in Lot 7; and the prepaid-parking window and the card-only
rule.

**On the trolley's schedule window**, the verifier ruled specifically that it is
publishable: *"Actual published times are 6:14/6:33 a.m. to 9:55/10:01 p.m.
depending on direction, which 'roughly 6:30 a.m. to 10 p.m.' fairly rounds — no
hard trip-duration claim is made, only a schedule window, so this does not trip
the duration ban."*

**On the prepaid-parking window**, the guide writes the cutoff in words rather
than digits. It is a purchase deadline rather than a journey, and it does not
fire the corpus's duration patterns; writing it out keeps it from reading as one
at a glance.

**No station, garage or lot becomes a place record.**
