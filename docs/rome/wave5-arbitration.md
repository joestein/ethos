## 0. THE PRINCIPLES, AND THE ONE THIS WAVE ADDS

Waves 1–4's six, unchanged:

- **P1 — Containment, not hosting.** Fronting, facing, adjoining or being famous for a monument is
  not owning it.
- **P2 — An institution's own statement of the unit beats a geometry query.** It cuts both ways.
- **P3 — Where nothing establishes containment, the place leaves the wave.**
- **P4 — A unit not in this wave, or already shipped, is a real owner.** Portuense, Della Vittoria,
  Appio-Latino, Salario, Tor di Quinto, Trieste and Q. XXXIV Levante all own what is theirs.
- **P5 — A building is placed by its own footprint, not by the length of its street.**
- **P6 — A street or square that *is* a boundary is contained by neither side.** Out as a place,
  usable as an address.

**P7 — THE OBJECT-LEVEL RULE, which Ardeatino's verifier asked for by name and which decides
eleven rulings below.** When a subject's *own* source names its unit, that governs over a polygon,
over a neighbour's list, and over a category. Where no object-level source names a unit, geometry
plus physical association decide. Where **two** object-level sources name **different** units,
neither governs and geometry breaks the tie. And a Wikipedia **category** is not an object-level
source — this wave proved it twice (Collina Fleming carries `Roma Q. II Parioli` and sits in
Municipio XV; the Ponte Milvio categories omit the very quartiere whose article lists the bridge).

Worked both ways in this file so nobody can call it convenient: it gives Flaminio Sant'Andrea del
Vignola against the polygon, gives Parioli three objects Pinciano and Flaminio wanted, and **takes
Santa Prassede away from Esquilino** (§2.3).

### 9.3 One priority-claim line, restated because five files reached for it

Wave 4 §9.1's line governs and I restate it once: **a sequence is publishable when the series is
bounded and named by the source** — "the first church on this site", "the fifteenth of Rome's rioni
by toponymic code", "the third from last quartiere in the official series", "one of the fifteen forts
built round Rome between 1877 and 1891", "at the IV mile". **A sequence is a banned priority claim
when the series is the city, the country or the world.** Both of the borderline cases in this wave
fall on the banned side and I have ruled them so at §10.8.

### 9.4 Trading durations — the rule, its two exits, and the eight deaths

**A founding year from an operator's own site IS a trading-duration claim and does not publish**
(wave 4 §9.7, programme-wide).

**Dead:** Fassi's «Dal 1880» — **and it would be a bad fact anyway: it.wikipedia's infobox reads
`data fondazione = 1880 (1928)` and the rione article says «attiva dal 1928»**; the British School's
«125 years» on its own site, anniversary framing and all; Baby Luna Park's «inaugurato il 29 giugno
1989»; the Porto Turistico's «25 Years Experience»; the Ostia Lido post office's «svolga questo
compito da **quasi 80 anni**»; the Teatro del Lido's "the spaces stood unused for over 25 years";
Al Biondo Tevere's founding year, whatever the site says; **the 1608 motu proprio of Paul V as the
origin of the San Paolo monastic library — a founding date for an institution taken from that
institution's own site, which is the exact shape the rule bans. The library publishes as a monastic
library; its origin date does not.**

**The two exits, both demonstrated in this wave and both to be used:**

1. **A third-party source attests the year.** The **Cinema dei Piccoli's 1934** build date comes from
   it.wikipedia, not from the operator, and publishes. So does the **Accademia Filarmonica Romana's
   1821** (§1.1 #10). So does **Roma Tre's 1992**, rewritten to *"The university has had premises in

   lives in the water tower's history record (E2), sourced to it.wikipedia**, and does not appear in
   the Ristorante Il Fungo record as a trading duration. **That distinction is what makes it
   publishable and it is worth learning.**

**The trading name itself always publishes verbatim, because naming a business is not asserting a
date.**

### 9.5 Research method — the single largest banned-language surface in this wave

**Pinciano §1.5 and §1.6, Flaminio §1's whole method table, and every "could not be fetched" note in
all nine files stay in the research files and reach no page.** None of the following may appear in
reader-facing text in any form, including softened: *point-in-polygon · polygon test · OSM relation ·
vertex · ring · bbox · ray-cast · control · margin in metres · rate-limited · HTTP 4xx/5xx · 429 ·
403 · 404 · 500 · did not resolve · could not be reached · re-fetched · during research · reverse
geocoding · footprint tests wholly inside · boundary method · for this guide · is published here ·
was obtained for this record.*

**The one permitted sentence, everywhere, is wave 4 §9.5's:**

> No source states its opening hours, so none are given here.

Substitute the field as needed — "its visiting hours", "its admission prices", "whether it can be
visited", "opening arrangements". **Never a reason, never a host, never a status code.** "No source
**available here** states…" and "No source **consulted here** states…" are research method wearing a
hat and are banned.

**Two constructions ARE allowed, because they state what an institution publishes rather than what we
did**, and both are needed in this wave: *"Roma Capitale gives the octagonal building a height of 18
metres; it.wikipedia gives 24.1 metres overall, of which 18.5 is the tower and 5.6 the off-centre
lantern turret"*, and *"The two accounts are not reconciled here."*

**Specific method leaks to strike, named so nobody argues:** Flaminio R9 and R65; Pinciano V-Z07 and
