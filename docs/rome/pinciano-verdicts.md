## 3. STRADDLER RULINGS

### S1 · Porta Pinciana — **ESCALATE, lean PUBLISH IN PINCIANO**

The finder's read is right and its evidence holds. Three independent points bear on it:

* `taken_slugs.py pinciana` → **0 matches**. Ludovisi is shipped with ten places, all inside the
  walls, and did not claim the gate. **No double-ownership is possible whichever way this falls.**
* The gate's **own article names no unit** — I confirmed this on re-fetch. There is no object-level
  source to beat Pinciano's own article, which lists it under `Porte nelle Mura aureliane`.
* Geometry is genuinely split at four metres and cannot decide it.

**Ruling: award to Pinciano, subject to arbitrator confirmation.** Facts confirmed and publishable:
one of the gates of the Aurelian Walls; enlarged by Honorius in **403**, when the two semicircular
flanking towers were added; earlier names *Porta Turata* and *Porta Salaria Vetus*; the medieval
"Porta Belisaria" story, which the source itself calls false. **Do not write «the gate is the hinge
point between three units» on the page** — that is boundary method. Write where it stands and stop.

### S2 · The Aurelian Walls, Porta Pinciana to piazza Fiume — **PUBLISH**

The finder asked whether any wall stretch may be a Pinciano place at all. The answer is mechanical
and the finder did not check it: **three shipped zones already ship wall segments** —
`celio-mura-aureliane-segment-rome`, `mura-aureliane-segment-testaccio-rome`,
`mura-aureliane-segment-trastevere-rome`. The precedent is settled. Campo Marzio owns the **Muro
Torto**, a specifically named stretch, not the Corso d'Italia frontage.

**Ruling: PUBLISH one record, slug `mura-aureliane-segment-pinciano-rome`,** confined to the frontage
from Porta Pinciana east to piazza Fiume. It must not annex the Muro Torto and must not describe how
the stretch was identified.

## 4. THE HOLY SEE PROPERTIES — the finder's analysis is adopted in full

The finder got the hardest ruling in this zone right, and it deserves saying plainly: **none of the
three Holy See–linked properties in Pinciano is a Lateran Treaty Art. 15 property**, and the
reasoning is sound. Rulings:

* **#79 Villa Giorgina — PUBLISH**, as the Apostolic Nunciature to Italy, a diplomatic mission
  standing on Italian soil. Status **closed**, on its own article's «L'edificio non è aperto al
  pubblico». **The page must not use the words *extraterritorial*, *Art. 15*, *Vatican territory* or
  *in the Vatican*.** The 1929-annexes argument that establishes this belongs in this file only —
  do not print the reasoning, print what the building is.
* **#81 Palazzo Borromeo — PUBLISH**, as Italy's embassy to the Holy See since 1929, Italian state
  property, on Italian soil, not open to the public. **DROP «l'unico caso di una sede diplomatica
  distaccata sul territorio dello stesso Paese che rappresenta» and «l'unica ambasciata al mondo a
  non godere del privilegio dell'extraterritorialità».** Both are superlatives and attribution does
  not save them. Exact replacement: *"Palazzo Borromeo has housed Italy's embassy to the Holy See
  since 1929. It was built in 1561 to a design by Pirro Ligorio for Pius IV, is owned by the Italian
  state, and is not open to the public."* The finder's own proposed wording added «a few hundred
  metres outside the walls» — **drop that clause**; it is an unsourced measurement doing the work of
  orientation by impression.
* **#80 Villa Elia — DROP as a standalone record.** One sentence, no date, no architect, no access.
  It may be named inside the Valle Giulia record as the seat of the Portuguese embassy to the Holy
  See. It is not a place.
* **#77 Palazzina Acerbo** is now the Venezuelan embassy. Same tier: a third-country mission on
  Italian soil. State the use; claim nothing about status.

## 7. TIER RULING — **GUIDE**, on **71** surviving places

44 PUBLISH plus 27 REWRITE, all of which ship with the wording supplied above.

I tested it the way wave 2 requires: **strike every contested object and the ruling does not move.**
Remove Porta Pinciana, the walls segment, Corso d'Italia, the Rinascente, the fountain of Julius III,
Explora and the Filarmonica — every escalation, all seven — and Pinciano still stands at 71, because
none of them was counted in the base. The four-place floor is not in play by an order of magnitude.

**I looked hard for the town-page answer and it is not here, and the reason is not thinness.** It is
concentration. Of the 71 survivors, **roughly 38 stand inside Villa Borghese** and a further nine are
the Valle Giulia institutes at its foot. That is a park page with a villino belt attached, not a town
with several centres of gravity. Trastevere shipped **63** and was ruled GUIDE; Testaccio shipped
**41** with four genuinely unrelated centres and was ruled GUIDE. Pinciano has more places than
Trastevere and fewer independent subjects. **GUIDE.**

**Three things the tier ruling must not be read as licence to paper over.**

First, **the anchor museum has no hours.** The Galleria Borghese is the draw named in the roster and
this corpus cannot tell a reader when it opens, because its own site does not serve them. The page
must say so in the corpus's honest formula and must not compensate by implying that timed entry means
anything about hours. Three other subjects are in the same position — the Japanese garden, the Museo
di Zoologia pending a re-fetch, and Villa Poniatowski.

Second, **a large fraction of the count is park furniture**, and the finder's warning about
over-shipping it is the most useful judgement in the file. I have enforced it: the fifty-odd fountains
become five records, the forty-odd donated monuments become one, the six gates become one, and the
thinnest follies merge. A writer who reverses those merges to inflate the count will produce a longer
page and a worse one.

Third, **three of the six great subjects are shut.** The mausoleum, the hypogeum and the catacombs
are closed, appointment-only or silent on access. Four separate honest access sentences is the true
shape of this quartiere, not a defect to be smoothed into implied opening hours.

## 8. WHAT THE FINDER GOT WRONG

Ranked by what would have shipped.

1. **The Villa Borghese opening hours.** The finder read a **contractor's gate-working shifts** as the
   park's opening hours and explicitly instructed the writer to prefer them over the source that
   actually states the park is open dawn to dusk. Two shifts a day is what that page describes. This
   would have told readers a public park is shut from 08.30 to 17.00.

2. **The Cinema dei Piccoli ticket price.** € 3,50 is a **summer promotion running 19 June to 20
   September 2026**, presented in the file as the price. Wrong for most of the year.

3. **The Casina di Raffaello hours are garbled, and two closures are missing.** «Tue–Fri,
   Sat/Sun/holidays 10:00–19:00» fuses two different closing times; the page says Tue–Fri **18:00**.
   The **9–23 August** and **1 January** closures were dropped.

4. **Three name collisions with shipped files, none of them checked.** Tempio di Antonino e Faustina
   and Arco di Settimio Severo both collide with Campitelli's Forum objects; the Fontane delle
   Tartarughe of Valle Giulia collide with Sant'Angelo's fountain in piazza Mattei. The finder ran
   `taken_slugs.py` on seven terms, all of them Borghese-flavoured, and on none of the three names it
   was about to duplicate. **A name collision fails the gate exactly as a slug collision does.**

5. **The Bioparco's Wednesday over-65 tariff is not on the page.** The € 9,00 is a last-minute promo
   sold two hours before closing. A small error, but it is an invented condition attached to a real
   price, which is the shape that survives audits.

6. **The official domain for the anchor museum is stale**, and the file instructs the writer to use
   it. `galleriaborghese.it` 301-redirects to `galleriaborghese.cultura.gov.it`.

7. **Two article-level records were fetched and never read** (#51 Sacro Cuore Immacolato, #52 Santa
   Teresa del Bambin Gesù) and one was cited without being fetched at all (the Battaglia di Valle
   Giulia article, and the Soprintendenza record for Anna Perenna). Citing a page nobody opened is the
   failure mode two previous waves flagged as a standing risk.

8. **A cross-reference points at the wrong record.** «the parish of Santa Teresa d'Avila (#28)» — #28
   is the Uccelliera; Santa Teresa d'Avila is #50.

9. **The finder's own replacement wording for Palazzo Borromeo reintroduced an impression** — «a few
   hundred metres outside the walls» — while fixing a superlative. Worth noting because it shows the
   corpus rules being applied to the source and then relaxed for the finder's own prose.

10. **«most visitors»** appears in the finder's S8 reasoning. It is banned language and it came from
    the finder, not from a source.

## 9. WHAT THE FINDER GOT RIGHT, and should be repeated

Said plainly because it is unusual: **this is the strongest boundary section any quartiere has
produced in this wave.** The finder cleared the Municipio bar with three independent sources rather
than assuming it; it recognised that Villa Borghese's own article settles the Pincio question and that
Campo Marzio was right to take the furniture; it refused to use the *zona urbanistica* layer as a
locator and said why; it flagged its own conflict of interest on Villa di Lucullo and gave the place
away; and its Lateran Treaty analysis of Villa Giorgina — a 1949 bequest cannot be in a 1929 annexe —
is the correct reading of a genuinely hard ruling and I have adopted it whole. It also escalated the
Guinness question rather than deciding it, which is exactly what an escalation is for.

Roughly two-thirds of the strikes in §6 were flagged by the finder before I got there. The failures
above are transcription and collision-checking failures, not judgement failures.
