### R1–R9 · MAXXI — Museo nazionale delle arti del XXI secolo · [museum] · **KEEP**

- **R1 PUBLISH** — Address via Guido Reni 4/A, 00196 Roma; a second entrance at via Guido Reni 8
  serving the bookshop and café. Museum's own site; identity and location, squarely publishable.
- **R2 PUBLISH** — Hours: closed Monday, Tuesday to Sunday 11.00–19.00, ticket desk closing an hour
  before the museum. Live from the operator.
- **R3 PUBLISH** — Admission, from the same page: €15 at the desk / €14 online full, €12 / €11
  reduced for ages 18–25 and groups of fifteen or more, free under 18; a fifteen-day MAXXI Replay
  ticket at the full-ticket price and an open ticket valid a year at €18; audio guides €3 in Italian
  and English. The finder omitted the prices; they are on the page he cited.
- **R4 PUBLISH** — MAXXI is in the Roma Pass circuit. Its own site says so.
- **R5 PUBLISH** — A national museum of 21st-century art and architecture run by the Fondazione
  MAXXI under the culture ministry, in two sections, MAXXI arte and MAXXI architettura.
- **R6 PUBLISH** — Competition announced July 1998 by the Soprintendenza Speciale Arte Contemporanea;
  273 entries, fifteen shortlisted; Zaha Hadid's scheme selected at the end of February 1999; the

- **R7 PUBLISH** — Built on the site of the disused ex caserma Montello; the long block beside the

- **R8 PUBLISH** — The museum and the Basilica di Santa Croce a Via Flaminia adjoin. Two structures

- **R9 DROP** — The finder's boundary line ("PIP → Flaminio … ~205 m inside the eastern edge … not

- *Note:* the MAXXI article carries Wikipedia's own `{{P|Toni celebrativi da ufficio stampa}}`

### R12–R13 · Museo Hendrik Christian Andersen · [museum] · **KEEP**

- **R12 PUBLISH** — Via Pasquale Stanislao Mancini 20; a state museum under the culture ministry,
  through the Polo museale del Lazio from December 2014 and the Direzione Musei statali di Roma from
  December 2019; built 1922–25 to Andersen's own design, a palazzina with an attached sculpture
  studio, neo-Renaissance elevations carrying heads of members of his family on the via Mancini and
  via Pisanelli fronts; Andersen died in 1940 leaving the house and collection to the state; the
  museum opened on 19 December 1999. The sculptures were carved for the *World Centre of
  Communication* he and Ernest Hébrard published in 1913. Attendance 10,803 in 2018, per the
  Ministero della Cultura's *Visitatori e introiti dei musei*.
- **R13 DROP** — **the opening hours and ticket prices (9.30–19.30, last entry 18.45, closed Mondays
  and 1 January / 1 May / 25 December; €6 full, €2 reduced).** I re-fetched the article and confirmed
  the citation is an archive.org capture dated **28 November 2007**. A nineteen-year-old archived
  ministry page is not a live hours source, and no `.cultura.gov.it` host is reachable to refresh it.
  The finder marked these `[UNVERIFIED — DO NOT SHIP AS FACT]` and was right.
  **Exact replacement wording:** *"No current source states the museum's opening hours or admission
  prices, so none are given here."* Note this sentence, not a sentence about archives or DNS.
- *One wording correction:* the article calls Lucia Andersen his *cognata*. Write "after the death of
  Lucia Andersen in 1978", without asserting the relationship, unless a writer re-reads the article.

### R14–R21 · Teatro Olimpico · [theater] · **KEEP**

- **R14 REWRITE** — the box-office hours. The site's sentence is a dated notice beginning «Da
  mercoledì 8 luglio sarà aperto…», not a standing schedule, and the summer closure carries no year.
  **Exact replacement wording:** *"The theatre's own site gives box-office hours of 9.30–13.00 and
  14.00–18.30, Monday to Friday, and a summer closure from 25 July to 23 August."* Attribute it to
  the theatre and give no year.
- **R15 PUBLISH** — Piazza Gentile da Fabriano 17, 00196 Roma. Operator's own contact page.
- **R16 PUBLISH** — 1,410 seats: 966 in the raked stalls and 444 in the horseshoe gallery; a stage
  about 10 m deep by 12 m wide; a removable timber floor and an orchestra pit. Counts and
  measurements, explicitly welcome under the corpus rules.
- **R17 PUBLISH** — The block was built in 1935–36 by the firm Garbarino, Sciaccaluga e Mezzacane for
  the Società Generale Immobiliare, which bought the property from the Banca Commerciale Italiana in
  1935; it ran as the Cine-Teatro Olimpico from 1936, mainly as a cinema with *avanspettacolo* in the
  intervals; the Accademia Filarmonica Romana leased it after the Second World War; Teatro Olimpico
  s.p.a. was formed in 1980 and bought the building, and the name and programme became Teatro
  Olimpico in 1981.
- **R18 PUBLISH** — The concert of 4 March 1965 conducted by Pierre Boulez, the Italian premiere of
  Stravinsky's *Abraham and Isaac* and the European premiere of his *Elegy for J.F.K.*
- **R19 REWRITE** — "A restoration recovered the original late-Rationalist architecture". Undated,
  and "recovered the original" is an evaluative claim about what was lost. **Exact replacement:**
  *"The building is late-Rationalist: reinforced concrete, with a travertine plinth and travertine
  window surrounds."*
- **R20 REWRITE** — the locator. The finder cites «sorgevano il quartiere Flaminio e il Foro Italico»
  as boundary evidence; that sentence describes the period's development and is **not** a location
  statement about the theatre. **Use the article's own structural description instead:** *"The block
  stands between piazza Gentile da Fabriano, lungotevere Flaminio, viale del Vignola and via Giorgio
  Vasari."* That is four named streets, not an impression.
- **R21 PUBLISH** — The Accademia Filarmonica Romana as the resident institution, folded in here per
  §4.2(f): founded in Rome in 1821 by amateur musicians led by Raffaele Muti Papazzurri, made an
  official institution of the Papal States in 1824, and a non-profit body under a presidential decree
  of 7 February 1969; it stages most of its events at this theatre. **The 1821 date publishes because
  it comes from a third-party article, not from the Accademia's own site** — the operator-founding-
  year rule is not triggered. Do not give the Accademia its own Flaminio record (see R57).
- **DROPPED, correctly quarantined by the finder:** «uno dei più importanti teatri storici di Roma»
  and «il secondo teatro di Roma e il settimo d'Italia per afflusso di spettatori» (AGIS 2009/10).
  Both are rankings. Attribution does not save them. Also «La zona è centrale e ben collegata …»
  (orientation by impression).

### R22–R29 · Basilica di Santa Croce a Via Flaminia · [historic-site] · **KEEP**

- **R22 REWRITE** — address. The finder gives "via Guido Reni" with no number and no number is
  sourced. **Publish the street alone:** *"On via Guido Reni."* Do not invent a number.
- **R23 PUBLISH** — A neo-Romanesque and neo-Byzantine basilica and a parish church.
- **R24 PUBLISH** — Built by the engineer Aristide Leonori at the wish of Pius X to mark the
  sixteenth centenary of the Edict of Milan of 313; works began 17 October 1912; opened 29 December
  1913; consecrated 21 May 1918 by Giuseppe Palica, titular archbishop of Philippi and vicegerent of
  the diocese of Rome.
- **R25 PUBLISH** — Raised to a parish on 19 March 1914 by the apostolic constitution *Quod iam
  pridem* and entrusted to the Stimmatine fathers.
- **R26 PUBLISH** — Made a minor basilica by Paul VI in 1964, and since 5 February 1965 the seat of
  the cardinalatial title *Santa Croce in Via Flaminia*, instituted by Paul VI. **These are
  ecclesiastical ranks conferred by the Holy See on its own church, not Italian state designations.**
  The evidence_ruling bans claims about *vincolo* and state protection; it does not reach a papal
  title. Publishes.
- **R27 PUBLISH** — A modern embroidered and jewelled reproduction of the Constantinian *labarum*.
- **R28 PUBLISH** — *"No source states its opening hours, so none are given here."* Status `open`.
- **R29 DROP** — the *Capella Santi Sposi*. OpenStreetMap tag only; no article and no second source.
  The finder said do not write it on OSM alone and he is right. Not a record.

### R30–R36 · Palazzo Marina · [historic-site] · **KEEP**

- **R30 PUBLISH** — A state building owned by the Ministry of Defence in the quartiere Flaminio, seat
  of the Regia Marina's coordinating department from its opening and today of the Navy, housing the
  Navy's central library. Two main entrances, on piazza della Marina off via Flaminia and on
  lungotevere delle Navi, with a service entrance on via Domenico Alberto Azuni.
- **R31 PUBLISH** — Commissioned from the architect Giulio Magni by the Ministry of the Navy; the
  commission dates to 1912, which is why the design keeps Roman Liberty, *barocchetto* and eclectic
  elements.
- **R32 REWRITE** — the inauguration. The date publishes (*La Stampa*, 29 October 1928, p. 1);
  Wikipedia's gloss that it fell on the sixth anniversary of the fascist regime is the editor's
  framing, not the newspaper's. **Exact replacement:** *"It was inaugurated on 28 October 1928."*
- **R33 PUBLISH** — A site of 31,000 m², of which 11,500 m² built over and 4,580 m² courtyards and
  gardens; 28 m high, 142 m long, six storeys plus an attic, about 750 rooms.
- **R34 PUBLISH** — The river-front entrance is flanked by the anchors of the Austro-Hungarian
  battleships *Tegetthoff* (left) and *Viribus Unitis* (right); brick and travertine, with reliefs,
  sculpture and glass on marine themes.
- **R35 DROP** — the Orson Welles *The Trial* filming claim. **Sourced only to IMDb Filming
  Locations, which is user-contributed.** The finder offered "attribute or drop"; the ruling is drop.
  Attribution does not cure a source that has no editorial control.
- **R36 REWRITE** — access. Do not cite the Sovrintendenza's 17 October 2018 education programme in
  reader-facing text — an event listing does not establish general access, and reasoning from it in
  prose is method. Status `open`. **Exact wording:** *"It is a working ministry building. No source
  states public opening hours, so none are given here."*

## 2. Zone-level prose

- **R60 REWRITE — the Municipio.** The finder's recommendation is upheld and tightened. Both sources
  (it.wiki's infobox, Nominatim's `display_name`) are aggregators. **The page may say
  "aggregators place the quartiere in Municipio Roma II" or say nothing. It may not assert it bare.**
  The Roma Capitale news item at R47 licenses "II Municipio" *for piazza Perin del Vaga only*.
- **R61 PUBLISH** — Q. I, the first of Rome's quartieri by toponymic code; code 201; zona urbanistica
  2C; named after the via Flaminia; among the first fifteen quartieri conceived in 1911 and
  officially instituted in 1921; arms azure, a flamen's *pileus* argent ornamented or (Pietrangeli,
  1953). "The first by toponymic code" is a position in an official series, not a superlative.
- **R62 PUBLISH** — The stated borders, quoted from the quartiere's own article footnote by footnote,
  and the fact that Flaminio is a left-bank quartiere. Write them as boundaries, not as instructions
  for walking them.
- **R63 PUBLISH** — The history: flat fields periodically flooded by the Tiber until the end of the
  19th century; the Società Automobili Roma's 1905 choice of the river bend for its plant; the 1911
  Esposizione nazionale and the sporting and cultural pole it fixed (Ippodromo dei Parioli, opened
  1911 and closed 1929; the Stadio Nazionale; the Valle Giulia pavilions); the First World War
  conversion of the Automobili Roma plant to the Reale Fabbrica di Armi; the 1960 Olympics and the
  work by Libera, Cafiero, Moretti, Monaco, Luccichenti, Vitellozzi and Nervi; the 1994 Auditorium
  competition, the MAXXI in 2010 and the ponte della Musica in 2011. **Several of these buildings are
  outside Q. I — write the history without implying the quartiere contains them.**

## 4. What the finder got wrong

Eight findings. The file is unusually honest and most of it survives; these are the places where it
does not.

1. **He applied his own 30-metre undecided band asymmetrically.** §1 says anything within ~30 m of
   the edge is *undecided and flagged, not resolved*. He then flagged the via Flaminia cluster at 4 m,
   7 m and 19 m as undecided — correct — but excluded **Explora at 11 m** outright in §5, and
   excluded the **Accademia Filarmonica's seat** on the parity rule alone. Same band, opposite
   treatment. Explora is now escalated (S12). The Accademia outcome is unchanged, but for the right
   reason: it belongs in the Teatro Olimpico record because that is where it performs, not because a
   house number decided it.
2. **He carried two CONI awards into a candidate record** (Circolo Canottieri Roma, *stella d'oro*
   1971 and *collare d'oro* 2019). Awards are banned. R54.
3. **He proposed publishing "about one kilometre downstream on the same bank"** while quarantining
   «a 300 metri da Piazza del Popolo» in §9 as banned proximity. The two are the same construction.
   R44.
4. **He deferred a fetch that succeeds.** `comune.roma.it` NWS376651 resolves in one call and
   confirms both the 2019 pedestrianisation and — usefully — the II Municipio for piazza Perin del
   Vaga from Roma Capitale's own mouth. Deferring it cost the file its single strongest municipal
   citation. R46, R47.
5. **He listed Ponte Milvio as a Flaminio candidate against his own evidence.** Categories, midpoint
   and the 17-of-20 object count all say Parioli. S1 recommends Parioli.
6. **He built a candidate record on a non-locator.** The Teatro Olimpico's «sorgevano il quartiere
   Flaminio e il Foro Italico» is about the period's development, not the theatre's address. R20.
7. **Four records rest on an OpenStreetMap tag and nothing else** — Cinema Tiziano, PalaLuiss, the
   Circolo Tennis della Stampa and the two gardens — and one of those carries a `kind` that does not
   fit (`stadium` for a university sports hall). All dropped, R55–R58.
8. **He reported the Teatro Olimpico's box-office hours as a standing schedule.** The page states
   them as a dated notice. R14 gives the wording that publishes.

**What he got right and should be credited for:** refusing to resolve the via Flaminia cluster;
