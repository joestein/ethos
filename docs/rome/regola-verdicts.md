## 1. THE ANCHORS

### 1.1 Palazzo Farnese (#1) — PUBLISH with four REWRITES

**PUBLISH as found:** the 1513 commission by Alessandro Farnese, later Paul III; work begun 1514,
halted by the 1527 Sack, resumed 1541, completed 1589; the four architects in sequence (Antonio
da Sangallo the Younger, Michelangelo 1546–49, Vignola 1569–73, Giacomo della Porta). Puccini's
second act of *Tosca*. Phone 06 686011.

**PUBLISH — the entire visiting block, re-verified line by line today.** Entry by guided tour
only, in Italian, French or English; tickets sold only through the official site, purchasable up
to 90 days ahead and until 5 days before; maximum 8 tickets per transaction; tickets are personal,
non-refundable and non-transferable unless the flexible ticket is bought. €15 Palazzo Farnese /
€20 Palazzo Farnese + Sotterranei / €22 Palazzo Farnese + École française de Rome; free for
children up to 6. Visitors present themselves 30 minutes before their slot with the original of
the same identity document used at purchase — identity card or passport only, driving licences
not accepted, photocopies not accepted. No cloakroom; trolleys, luggage and backpacks may not be
brought in, women's handbags may. No photography inside; phones on silent and not used for calls.
Groups are a maximum of 33, and 25 for the Tuesday, Thursday and Friday 17.00 visits. Accessible
to visitors with disabilities except the École française de Rome and the Sotterranei; the
Sotterranei are not open to children under 12 or to visitors with motor disabilities.

**PUBLISH:** the Sotterranei opened to the public on 29 May 2024 and hold two black-and-white
mosaic pavements, one of sea animals and monsters, one of circus acrobats on horseback, the
*desultores*. Confirmed verbatim.

**REWRITE 1 — the Carracci dates.** The finder wrote 1597–1604 with Domenichino and Lanfranco.
The palace's own site says 1597–1608 and names only the two brothers. Use verbatim:

> The Galleria Carracci was painted by the brothers Annibale and Agostino Carracci. The palace's
> official visits site dates the work 1597–1608 and describes a vault of 20 metres frescoed with
> subjects from Ovid's *Metamorphoses*.

Domenichino and Lanfranco may be named only if the author re-fetches an it.wikipedia or
Roma Capitale page that states it, and attributes it there.

**REWRITE 2 — the Sotterranei schedule, which has moved.** The finder's Monday/Tuesday/Thursday
reading is what the **ticket page** says today, and it carries an explicit "Dal 2026" notice. The
palace's other page still says "tutti i martedì in lingua inglese e tutti i giovedì in lingua
italiana e francese". The official site contradicts itself. The ticket page is the one that sells
the ticket and the one that is dated; it governs. Use verbatim:

> Palazzo Farnese: Monday 15.00, 16.00 and 17.00; Tuesday, Thursday and Friday 15.00 and 16.00.
> Palazzo Farnese with the Sotterranei: Monday 17.00 in Italian, Tuesday 17.00 in English,
> Thursday 17.00 in French. Palazzo Farnese with the École française de Rome: Friday 17.00.

**REWRITE 3 — the "quattro meraviglie". DROP the whole construction.** The finder argued it is a
named historical grouping, not a ranking we assert. I rule against that. "One of the four wonders
of Rome" has the exact shape the corpus bans, attribution does not save it, and — decisively —
**the finder's three companion items are unsourced**. The official page says only "Anticamente
considerato una delle 'quattro meraviglie di Roma'" and names nothing else; the Borghese cembalo,
the Caetani staircase and the Carboniani portal appear in no source the finder fetched. Drop the
grouping and drop the list. Also DROP, as the finder correctly flagged: "uno degli edifici più
belli ed importanti di Roma", "il suo capolavoro", "la più perfetta conclusione", "Tra i più bei
palazzi del Cinquecento romano", "il palazzo gentilizio più importante".

**REWRITE 4 — ownership and tenure.** The 1936 lease to France for 99 years is on it.wikipedia
and is NOT on the official site the finder cited alongside it. Use verbatim what the institution
says:

> The palace has housed the Embassy of France since 1874 and, from the following year, the École
> française de Rome.

The 1936 lease may be added only with an attribution to whichever page actually carries it.

**Note for the author, not for the page:** the ticket page states tour lengths (60 and 90 minutes,
plus 30 minutes of security checks). These are the venue's own statement about its own product,
not a trip duration, and are publishable if the author wants them. They are not required.

### 1.3 Palazzo Spada (#3) — PUBLISH with REWRITE

### 1.4 The Borromini perspective (#4) — PUBLISH, discrepancy RESOLVED

The finder asked me to settle 8.82 m against Turismo Roma's "solo 9 metri". **I settle it: 8.82 m,
and Turismo Roma's rounded figure does not appear anywhere on the page.** Two numbers for one
colonnade in one corpus is worse than either alone. I re-fetched the wikitext and confirm every
figure. Use verbatim:

> Borromini built the colonnade in a single year, between 1652 and 1653, assisted by the
> Augustinian Giovanni Maria da Bitonto. It replaced a painted perspective by Giovanni Battista

> floor rises, the ceiling drops, and the planes converge on a single vanishing point. The cast of

**PUBLISH:** reached at the end of the first-floor museum route, through the Giardino Segreto;
a 200-lire coin of 1990 and a €0.60 stamp of 2011 carry it.

### 1.5 Piazza Farnese (#5) — PUBLISH
Paved from 1545; eight streets and alleys run into it; Palazzo Farnese, Santa Brigida and its
convent, Palazzo Fusconi Pighini, Palazzo Mandosi Mignanelli at the vicolo de' Venti corner, which
takes its name from a family and not from wind. Used for tournaments, bullfights, popular
festivals and summer floodings.

### 1.6 The two fountains of piazza Farnese (#6) — PUBLISH with one REWRITE
**PUBLISH:** two Roman granite basins moved from piazza San Marco, where they had stood since
1466 — Paul III took the first, Cardinal Alessandro Farnese the second some forty years later.
Girolamo Rainaldi turned them into fountains in 1626, after Gregory XV granted the Farnese 40
*once* of Acqua Paola water by a document dated 1 September 1621. Travertine outer basin,
amphora-shaped baluster, smaller upper basin, Farnese lily, seven jets each. Restored: the
southern one 1938–39, both 1992–93, the southern one again 2007.

**REWRITE — the provenance of the basins.** Use verbatim:

> Their origin is usually given as the Terme di Caracalla, though it.wikipedia records that the
> question is not settled.

### 1.7 Via Giulia (#7) — PUBLISH, ESCALATED
Commissioned by Julius II from Donato Bramante, opened 1508; 950 m; sampietrini; from piazza San
Vincenzo Pallotti to piazza dell'Oro; also called *via Magistralis*. The three stated purposes.
Cut off from the river by the lungotevere embankments from 1888. Fascist-era demolitions between
via della Barchetta and vicolo delle Prigioni, partly filled by the Liceo Virgilio. 2008 as the
500th anniversary. **See §5.1 for the ownership ruling and the wording that goes with it.**

### 1.8 Fontana del Mascherone (#8) — PUBLISH with one DROP
Re-fetched and confirmed verbatim, including **"Rione: Regola"** in Roma Capitale's own file — the
single strongest membership statement in the whole finder file. PUBLISH: Girolamo Rainaldi
(1570–1655), attributed; dated to the second quarter of the seventeenth century; grey granite,
white marble, travertine, metal; originally fed by the acquedotto Paolo; a fountain for "Strada
Giulia" assigned by the Congregazione sopra le fonti as early as 1570 and intended for Acqua
Vergine, but the work waited on the Acqua Paola channel of 1612; built at Farnese expense, as the
lily crowning it indicates. Originally free-standing in a small widening that held an open-air
theatre in 1660; the wall behind it went up at the end of the nineteenth century. Antique granite
thermal basin in a slightly sunken paved catch-basin; marble backing crowned by a metal lily; the
antique mask pours the water. Turismo Roma's 1720 story — the fountain ran wine for three
consecutive days for Marco Antonio Zondadari's appointment as Grand Master of the Order of Malta —
PUBLISHES, attributed to Roma Capitale.

**DROP the OSM `start_date=1626`.** It contradicts the Sovrintendenza's own dating and OSM does
not outrank Roma Capitale on the dating of a Roman fountain. The Sovrintendenza's "second quarter
of the seventeenth century" is the only dating that appears.

**Turismo Roma's ~300 m coordinate error is a research-file fact and must not reach the page.**

I record that several of these are certainly real and several are probably good. That is not the

## 7. WHAT THE FINDER GOT RIGHT

Stated plainly, because adjudication is not the same as fault-finding.

- **The rione reassignments are correct and valuable.** The Museo Criminologico, the Oratorio del
  Gonfalone and Palazzo Sacchetti are Ponte's, against the brief's own "likely subjects" list.
  Working from the OSM boundary relation rather than a rate-limited geocoder was the right call and
  is stronger evidence than reverse geocoding.
- **The designation ruling was obeyed completely.** Not one *vincolo*, listing or protection claim
  anywhere in 96 candidates. No UNESCO claim for the rione. No Lateran Treaty confusion, and an
  explicit statement that the English College, Santa Maria in Monserrato and San Tommaso di
  Canterbury are pontifical institutions on Italian soil and not extraterritorial properties.
- **The superlative discipline is good.** The finder flagged roughly twenty banned phrases from
  Roma Capitale, it.wikipedia and official sites, including several it would clearly have liked to
  keep. I found three it missed, all priority claims buried mid-article: the Carceri Nuove "first
  modern penitentiary in Rome", the Scuderie "first large stables ever found", and Sant'Eligio's
  via Giulia as "one of the most prestigious addresses in Rome".
- **The Santa Caterina da Siena trap** — a Turismo Roma page for a different church on via
  Populonia, plus a Wikidata record with a wrong address pointing at a church Monti already owns —
  is the kind of thing that ships a collision if nobody looks. Catching it was good work.
- **Flagging its own weakest three** (Arco Farnese, Palazzo dei Tribunali, Santa Lucia del
  Gonfalone) meant I spent my time on the right questions. Two of the three I dropped or escalated
  exactly as invited.

## 8. WHAT THE FINDER GOT WRONG

- **The collision check was incomplete and its conclusion was overstated.** Ponte Giuseppe Mazzini
  is already Trastevere's. The search terms never included "ponte" or "mazzini", yet the file
  concludes "Only three hits touch Regola's territory". Four do. The brief exists because a
  hand-written reservation list let a collision through in an earlier wave; a partial mechanical
  check with a confident conclusion is the same failure wearing a script.
- **It published a narrower date than its own source supports** on the Carracci Gallery, 1597–1604
  against the official site's 1597–1608, without flagging the difference — while flagging a dozen
  other conflicts scrupulously.
- **It mis-described the Galleria Spada hours evidence** as "two independent readings" when Roma
  Capitale gives no opening span at all, then carried an OSM string into the record after writing
  the rule that forbids it. The same OSM-hours drift runs through six church records.
- **It asserted a four-item list** for the *quattro meraviglie* when its source names one item, and
  argued a ranking into publishability on the strength of it.
- **It attributed the Ospizio facts to "two places"** when one page carries them, and attributed
  the civico 113 fact to a page that does not contain it. Both facts survive; the provenance did
  not.
- **It carried an unfetched PDF's number** (63,099 visitors) as "a publishable number not a
  ranking". The number may well be right. It was read off an infobox.

## 9. TIER RULING

**FULL GUIDE. 75 surviving places, of which 63 are unconditional and 12 turn on straddler
rulings.**

The honest floor is **63**: that is the count if every one of the eight escalated straddlers is
decided against Regola and all four contingent business records go with them. The honest ceiling
is 75. Either number is a full-guide rione by a wide margin, and the tier does not change on the
arbitrator's decisions — which is the right state for a tier ruling to be in.

The composition is what makes it a guide rather than a list:

| Group | Surviving | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Anchors | 8 | Farnese, Spada, the Galleria, the Borromini perspective, the piazza, its fountains, via Giulia, the Mascherone |
| Churches and ex-churches | 24 | 21 standing or surviving in fabric, plus the Ospizio, the Stanze and the English College |
| Palaces and civic buildings | 18 | including Carceri Nuove and the Scuderie di Augusto |
| Streets, squares, bridges, water | 6 | after five demotions to context |
| Eating, drinking, sleeping, shopping | 19 | after 24 unsourced drops |
| **Total** | **75** | |

The rione carries four hard-sourced access regimes that most Rome units cannot offer — Palazzo
Farnese's full ticketing and security rules, Sant'Eligio's booked weekday mornings, Santa Maria
dell'Orazione e Morte's appointment-only crypt, and the Galleria Spada's last admission — plus a
run of national churches (Spain, England, Sweden, Naples, Bologna, Siena) that gives the guide a
spine no other rione in this wave has.

The drop rate is high — 50 records — and almost all of it is one class: businesses and thin
addresses known only from an Overpass dump. That is a finder-side collection choice, not a
shortage in Regola. Nothing shippable was lost.
