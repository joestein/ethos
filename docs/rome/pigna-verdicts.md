## 2. Five things the finder got wrong

### 2.1 The Palazzo Maffei Art. 15 attribution is not established, and vatican.va is reachable

The finder wrote that the permitted phrasing is "*that Palazzo Maffei Marescotti is one of the
properties named in Art. 15 of the Lateran Treaty*", and flagged as an open question whether the
grant survives the Vicariate's 1964 move. Both halves need fixing.

I fetched the Holy See's own list at
`https://www.vatican.va/news_services/press/documentazione/documents/sp_ss_scv/informazione_generale/extraterritoriale_it.html`
(page stamped *Aggiornamento: 03.04.2001*). Item 9 reads, verbatim:

> "il Palazzo Maffei o del Vicariato (già sede della Curia diocesana di Roma, che vi ha tuttora
> alcuni uffici)"

Three consequences.

- **The grant still attaches.** The list is prefaced "i beni extraterritoriali della Santa Sede in
  Roma sono attualmente". Open question closed, in the finder's favour.
- **But the article number is not sourced.** The same preface says the list takes account of
  "*i vari accordi sottoscritti al riguardo posteriormente tra la Santa Sede e l'Italia in date
  diverse*" — later agreements, not only 1929. it.wikipedia's *Zone extraterritoriali* article
  annotates this building "**da aprile 2014**", which if true means the grant is not the 1929 one.
  The English Treaty text the finder read says "the Palace of the Vicariate"; nothing I fetched
  establishes that that 1929 phrase denotes this building. **No Pigna page may cite Art. 15 for
  Palazzo Maffei, or give 1929 as its date.** Cite vatican.va's list and stop there.
- **The finder over-corrected on the Vicariate.** They wrote that "a page that says 'the Vicariate
  of Rome is here' would be wrong by sixty-two years." The Holy See says the diocesan Curia
  **still has some offices in the building**. The accurate statement is that it is no longer the
  *seat*, not that it has gone.

Note also that vatican.va's own summary sentence merges the two privileges — "godono del privilegio
della extraterritorialità **e sono esenti da espropriazione e da tributi**". That is the Holy See
describing its own properties and is not our conflation of Arts. 15 and 16; it is a further reason
to cite the list rather than the treaty articles.

**One incidental gain:** vatican.va states that all the Rome extraterritorial properties bar the
Seminario Romano Minore are inscribed on the World Heritage list. That is a UNESCO inscription
statement, explicitly publishable under ruling 1, and it is *not* a designation claim.

### 2.2 The BiASA closure notice does not exist — SERIOUS

The finder wrote, in bold: *"CURRENT NOTICE: VIVE's news index carries 'BiASA: chiusura della sede
di Palazzo Venezia a partire dal 7 settembre 2026' — the Palazzo Venezia site closes 7 Sept 2026.
Any hours we publish must account for this."*

I fetched `vive.cultura.gov.it/it/news` and enumerated every occurrence of "BIASA" on it. There
are four. None is that notice. What the index actually carries is the **opposite**:

> "Biblioteca di Archeologia e Storia dell'Arte (BiASA): **riapertura al pubblico dal 28 aprile
> 2025** — La sede di Palazzo Venezia è stata riaperta al pubblico"

preceded by the February 2025 item announcing the works ("*rimarrà chiusa al pubblico fino al
27 aprile 2025*"). The only later BiASA item is a Christmas opening-hours variation. The single
"7 settembre" string on the page belongs to an Altare della Patria item about a restoration-site
visit. **The claim is dropped.** A corpus that had shipped it would have told readers a working
national library was closing on a date invented from a neighbouring headline. This is the one
finder error I would call dangerous rather than merely loose, and it is the reason I re-fetched
fourteen sources instead of five.

### 2.3 Santa Maria sopra Minerva's own site is a Wikipedia mirror

The finder flagged this as "**a rare case of a place's own site attesting its rione**" and leaned
on it for the Pigna attribution. It is not. The site's lead is copied word for word from
it.wikipedia, down to the Latin gloss and the trailing clause:

> site: "…è una basilica di Roma situata nel rione Pigna, in piazza della Minerva, nelle vicinanze
> del Pantheon."
> it.wikipedia: "…è una basilica minore di Roma situata nel [[…|rione Pigna]], in [[piazza della
> Minerva]], nelle vicinanze del [[Pantheon (Roma)]]."

It is aggregator content wearing an official domain. The rione attribution for the Minerva rests on
the finder's 38/38 geometry, which is sound and sufficient — but the corpus must not present the
basilica as having attested its own rione. (Two further notes: it.wikipedia's infobox gives the
official site as `basilicaminerva.it`, a *different* domain from the one the finder used; and
"nelle vicinanze del Pantheon" is vague proximity and is banned regardless of who wrote it.)

### 2.4 The Sovrintendenza's talking-statue section is live, not 404

The finder recorded that the *Statue parlanti* navigation entry "404s" and that "the nav
over-promises". It does not. The section resolves at
Home » Patrimonio » Roma antica » Statue parlanti, and **Madama Lucrezia has her own institutional
page** at `sovraintendenzaroma.it/content/statua-detta-di-madama-lucrezia`. See §3.

### 2.5 Smaller misreadings

- **Obelisco della Minerva, date.** The finder gives 4th c. BC from it.wikipedia. Roma Capitale's
  own tourism page for the Minerva says **VI secolo a.C.** The two sources conflict; the date is
  dropped (§4 #72). Bernini's 1667 design, Ercole Ferrata's execution and the Iseo Campense
  provenance are corroborated by both and survive.
- **Madama Lucrezia, subject.** The finder, from it.wikipedia, calls it "possibly originally a
  statue of Isis". The Sovrintendenza says "*forse ritratto di Faustina e proveniente dal Tempio di
  Iside*" — possibly a portrait of Faustina, *from* the Temple of Isis. Different claim.
- **"Nanni di Baccio Bigio."** The Gesù's own site spells it **Biggio**. Use the site's spelling.
- **Area Sacra inauguration.** The Sovrintendenza says it was inaugurated on 21 April 1929
  **by Benito Mussolini**. The finder dropped the agent. Not an error, but the omission makes the
  sentence read as if the site simply opened.

## 3. Two finder rejections I overturn

The finder marked the **Fontana del Facchino** (#74) as having "no institutional source" because
"Sovrintendenza's 44-fountain index does NOT include it", and treated **Madama Lucrezia** (#75) as
aggregator-only because the Statue parlanti URL 404'd. Both are wrong. Site search finds both, at
Home » Patrimonio » Roma medioevale e moderna » Fontane and Home » Patrimonio » Roma antica »
Statue parlanti respectively. The Facchino page is stamped 17/02/2026 and carries a full history:

> "La fontana detta in antico il barile o mezzo barile, fu realizzata a spese e su disegno del
> pittore fiorentino Jacopino del Conte … ad ornamento della sua casa di via del Corso, tra il 1580
> … e il 1598 anno della sua morte. Dopo la demolizione della casa, avvenuta nel 1724, la fontana
> venne ricollocata sul prospetto principale di Palazzo De Carolis-Simonetti e nel 1872-1873
> spostata su via Lata per ragioni di viabilità."

That single page upgrades **three** records: the Facchino itself, Madama Lucrezia (both named as
members of the "Congresso degli arguti"), and **Palazzo De Carolis** (#37), which the finder had as
a bare it.wikipedia list entry and which now has Roma Capitale attesting a dated event on its
façade. A finder who searches an institution's site rather than trusting its navigation gets
better yield; I record this so wave 3 does it.

I also found a dedicated Sovrintendenza page for the **Torre del Papito** (#16) —
`/content/la-torre-del-papito` — giving the Papareschi attribution, the 14th century, the Anacleto
II "Papetto" etymology, the Foschi de Judeis / Boccamazza / Cesarini succession and the Nolli plan.

## 6. Tier ruling

**GUIDE.** Not close.

The spine is the Pantheon, the Area Sacra with its four Republican temples and the Torre del

large minority of the 131 to fail verification" and was too optimistic — two thirds failed. That
is the right shape for a rione this famous and this thinly documented per-property, and it is the
confirmation-rate signal the brief asked for working as intended.
