114xx" is a postal address, "greater Jamaica" is a CD12 cluster, and "Jamaica" is a neighborhood.**

conflicts. Jamaica Hills and Jamaica Estates are different: both are *separate* neighborhoods
sitting **north of Hillside Avenue**, and Hillside Avenue is CD12's northern boundary. A place north
of Hillside Avenue is not in Jamaica.

### Is Jamaica adjacent to Kew Gardens at all?

**Ruling: at a corner, and not usefully.** Confirmed verbatim: Kew Gardens is *"bounded to the north
by the Union Turnpike and the Jackie Robinson Parkway, to the east by the Van Wyck Expressway and

**Jamaica is not named.** The Jamaica article's own border sentence names *"Kew Gardens Hills"* —
a different place — and not Kew Gardens. CD12's northern edge (Hillside Avenue) and western edge
(Van Wyck) meet at the one corner that is also Kew Gardens' southeast corner. **The two pages share
no place. Do not manufacture an adjacency sentence.** Richmond Hill *is* genuinely adjacent:
*"The area borders Kew Gardens and Forest Park to the north, Jamaica and South Jamaica to the east,
South Ozone Park to the south, and Woodhaven and Ozone Park to the west."* Both pages may say so;
neither may claim Jamaica Avenue whole.

### Why not (c) — the gate is not wrong

I read the gate before ruling. `test/ethos/seeds/queens_seed_data_test.exs`, `@marquee_institutions`
and *"each marquee queens institution appears in exactly one neighborhood"*.

The assertion is `marquee_file_count(...) == 1`, and `marquee_file_count/1` counts **distinct
files**, not places. It therefore asserts *"exactly one seed file carries a record for this
institution."* **It never asserted containment.** The prompt's framing — that *"no neighborhood may
claim it"* and *"no file may host its record"* are different statements — is exactly right, and the
gate only ever made the second one. So there is nothing in the gate that the Forest Park and Alley
Pond Park rulings contradict. Rewriting it to exempt "institutions ruled to no neighborhood" would
delete the omission half of a deliberately symmetric assertion for **precisely the two parks the
comment names as the ones most likely to fall between waves** (*"Forest Park runs through Woodhaven,
Richmond Hill, Glendale and Kew Gardens ... Alley Pond Park through Bayside, Douglaston and Oakland
Gardens"*). That is weakening it to make a failure go away, and the file's own instruction forbids
the shape of edit: *"WIDEN the pattern ... never delete a row, which is the one edit that silently
restores the gap."*

There **is** a carve-out precedent in the file, and it cuts the other way. The Citi Field places are
*"DELIBERATELY ABSENT"* from the list because they are code-owned and *"appear in ZERO seed files,
so listing them here would make this test report all eight as missing forever."* Jamaica Bay
Wildlife Refuge is excluded for the same reason — both its neighborhoods are `in_scope: false`.
That carve-out is for institutions the corpus **structurally cannot host**. Alley Pond Park is not
one: `bayside.json` and `douglaston.json` both shipped, both already carry Alley Pond Park prose,
and either could carry the record. The precedent does not reach it.

### Why not (b) — deleting forest-park is a net loss

`forest-hills.json`'s `forest-park` record carries `town: "Queens"`, a real street address
(`80-30 Park Lane, Queens, NY 11415`), NYC Parks flagship acreage and ZIP list, and a `history`
field that says in terms *"The park is not administratively part of any neighborhood"* with the
QN8291 joint-interest-area reasoning. That record is **true, sourced, address-bearing and useful**,
and deleting it would leave `/p/forest-park` a 404 for the borough's most-walked park while making
the gate vacuous for both rows. (b) destroys correct information to buy consistency. Reject.
