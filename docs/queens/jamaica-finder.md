   Where I am summarising rather than reproducing an extracted quote I write **(paraphrase)**.

## THE NAME "JAMAICA" COLLIDES IN AT LEAST FIVE DIRECTIONS — RESOLVE BEFORE RECORDING

This is the single largest hazard in this file. Every one of the following is a *different thing*
and sources use the bare word "Jamaica" for all of them:

- **N1. The neighborhood** — Jamaica, Queens, Community District 12.
- **N2. Jamaica Bay** — Wikipedia: *"Bay on the southern side of Long Island, New York"*, *"primarily
  divided between the boroughs of Brooklyn and Queens in New York City, with a small part touching
  Nassau County."* On the origin of the name it says **"The name derives from the nearby town (now
  neighborhood) of Jamaica."** The extraction explicitly noted that the article **does not state
  that the bay physically borders the Jamaica neighborhood.** The bay is miles south, past South
  Jamaica, Springfield Gardens and JFK. **Nothing on Jamaica Bay belongs on this page.**
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_Bay
- **N3. Jamaica Avenue** — a 12-mile street that starts in **Brooklyn** and runs through Woodhaven,
  Richmond Hill, Jamaica, Hollis and Queens Village. See F30–F33. The avenue is not the
  neighborhood, and most of its length is outside it.
- **N4. Jamaica station** — the LIRR/AirTrain hub. It is *in* the neighborhood, but it is a regional
  facility whose 200,000 weekday riders overwhelmingly never set foot in the neighborhood.
- **N5. The country of Jamaica** — Queens has a large Caribbean population and sources about
  Jamaican-Americans in Queens are not sources about Jamaica, Queens. I recorded nothing from this
  category, but the verifier should watch for it.

Additional trap: **the Jamaica neighborhood is not Jamaica Bay's shore, and the two are separated by
the entire southern half of Community District 12 plus JFK Airport.** Per the borough brief, the
Rockaway pages sit across that bay and share nothing with this page.

### B. History, naming, colonial origins — a rich vein

**F13.** Dutch founding: *"Peter Stuyvesant dubbed the area Rustdorp ('rest-town') in granting the
1656 land patent."*
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica,_Queens

**F14.** Etymology, version 1: *"Jamaica may get its name from Yameco, a corruption of a word for
'beaver' in the Lenape language."* Note the hedge **"may"** — the source does not assert it.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica,_Queens

**F15.** Etymology, version 2 (a *different* account, from the Jamaica Avenue article):
*"the English, who took control of the colony in 1664, renamed the little settlement 'Jameco', for
the Jameco (or Yamecah) Native Americans."* **F14 says the name comes from a Lenape word for
beaver; F15 says it comes from the name of a people. These are not the same claim.** Verifier must
not blend them.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_Avenue

**F16.** County seat: *"In 1683, Jamaica became the county seat of Queens County, one of the original
counties of New York."* **This is a strong candidate for the page's historical spine.** (Note the
county seat is today at Queens Borough Hall in **Kew Gardens** — see C17.)
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica,_Queens

**F17.** Pre-colonial trade route: *"Jamaica Avenue was part of a pre-Columbian trail for tribes from
as far away as the Ohio River and the Great Lakes, coming to trade skins and furs for wampum."*
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_Avenue

**F18.** Turnpike era: by the 1800s, *"the old road through Jamaica Pass was the Brooklyn Ferry Road;
at mid-century this became the Brooklyn and Jamaica Plank Road."*
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_Avenue

**F19.** Prospect Cemetery *"established in 1668"* and referred to as the *"burring plas"* [sic — the

not an extraction error].
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prospect_Cemetery_(Queens)

**F20.** 20th-century commercial decline: *"In the 1960s and 1970s, many big box retailers moved to

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica,_Queens

**F21.** Slum clearance and public housing: *"In 1939, the city began slum clearance projects in the

**Note: this is from the SOUTH JAMAICA article, about South Jamaica.**
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Jamaica,_Queens

**F22.** The New Great Migration: *"Foreclosures and the high level of unemployment of the 2000s and

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica,_Queens

**F23.** 2007 rezoning: in 2005 *"the New York City Department of City Planning drafted a plan that

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica,_Queens

**F24.** Greater Jamaica Development Corporation: *"Formed in 1967, Greater Jamaica Development

https://www.gjdc.org/about-us/

**F30.** Rufus King the man: *"He was a delegate from Massachusetts to the Continental Congress and

**F32.** Death and burial: King *"died at the mansion on April 29, 1827"* (King Manor article); the

**This is the strongest link between King Manor and Grace Episcopal Church — and I could not reach
the Grace Church article to corroborate it. High-value verification target.**
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rufus_King

**F33.** Rufus King Park: *"a city block bounded by Jamaica Avenue to the south, 150th Street to the

### G. Parks

**F56.** **Captain Tilly Park**: *"165th Street to the west, 85th Avenue to the north, Chapin Parkway
and Gothic Drive to the northeast, and Highland Avenue to the south"*, *"9.16-acre (3.71 ha)."*
Goose Pond is *"[a] 2-acre (0.81 ha) lake in the center"* and the park's *"centerpiece"*; it is

*"Upland Park"* in 1912, changed to *"Captain Tilly Park"* in early 1935 and *"the renamed park was
dedicated that August."* In the 1990s *"seven underwater air pumps were added"* for water quality.
**The kettle-pond geology is a genuinely distinctive detail. See C8 on the neighborhood assignment.**
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_Tilly_Park

**F57.** **Baisley Pond Park**: *"Public park located in the southeastern part of Queens, New York

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baisley_Pond_Park
