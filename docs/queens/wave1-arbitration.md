# Queens Wave 1 — ARBITRATION RULING

**Scope:** Astoria · Ditmars-Steinway · Long Island City. Three authors write in parallel and cannot
see each other's work. This file is binding on all three.

**Inputs read in full:** `astoria-verdicts.md`, `ditmars-steinway-verdicts.md`,
`long-island-city-verdicts.md`.

## The evidence ladder I applied

1. **The place's own street address, with the neighborhood name in it.** Self-published or
   primary-City address lines outrank everything else.
2. **What the best-evidenced source says explicitly**, in prose, about where the place *is*.
3. **Community-district and boundary definitions the verdicts confirm** — used only to break ties.

Three corollaries I applied consistently, and which authors should not re-litigate:

- **Community Board 1 is non-probative in this wave.** CB 1 covers Astoria *and* northern Long
  Island City; all three verdict files confirm this independently. No ruling below rests on CB 1.
- **NTA `QN0101` is non-probative for Astoria vs. Ditmars-Steinway.** DCP fuses the two into one
  tabulation area, `Astoria (North)-Ditmars-Steinway`. It cannot discriminate, in either direction.
- **A naming fact is not a location fact.** A station called Ditmars, a street called Steinway, a
  tunnel called Steinway, a memorial to the sons of Long Island City — none of these move a place.

Where a designation report's **header label** conflicts with an explicit **body sentence** in the
same document, the body sentence wins. Where no body sentence contradicts it, the header label
governs. This one principle decides Lawrence Family Graveyard, Steinway Mansion and Lent Homestead
in different directions, and it is deliberate.

**Ruling on the standing question the Ditmars-Steinway verifier reserved for me:** *the "Steinway"
label used by LPC designation reports maps onto the Ditmars-Steinway file*, and only that label
does. "Steinway Street", "Steinway Place" and "Steinway Tunnel" are names, not labels, and carry
nothing.

### The two close calls, and what would overturn them

**Steinway Mansion → Ditmars-Steinway.** This overrules two sibling verifiers who both ruled Astoria,
and I want the reasoning on the record. Their Astoria ruling rests on a **tertiary** source
(Wikipedia's opening sentence and infobox, `18-33 41st Street, Astoria, Queens, New York 11105`).
The competing evidence is a **primary City designation report** whose address line reads
`18-33 41st Street, Steinway, Borough of Queens`, and which never uses the word Astoria. At rank 1
both are addresses carrying a neighborhood name, so source tier breaks the tie, and LPC wins. The
DCP tabulation area is named `Astoria (North)-Ditmars-Steinway` and is compatible with either.
**Overturned by:** any current primary source — an LPC amendment, a PLUTO or DCP neighborhood field,
or the property's owner — giving this address an Astoria neighborhood line. PLUTO as confirmed gives
`zip 11105`, `cd 401`, `yearbuilt 1858` and no neighborhood, so it does not settle it.
**Note the deliberate split:** the factory goes to Astoria and the mansion to Ditmars-Steinway. That
is not an oversight. The factory has a current self-published address naming Astoria; the mansion has
none, so its primary label governs.

**Sohmer & Company Piano Factory → Astoria.** LP-2172's prose is explicit and repeated ("one of the
most prominent structures along the Queens side of the East River in Long Island City"), and it is
the better-written source. But rank 1 outranks rank 2, and at rank 1 the address `31-01 Vernon Blvd`
carries `city: "Astoria"` in DCLA and sits in NTA `QN0102 Old Astoria-Hallets Point`. The LPC report
hedges itself — "in the northern part of Long Island City near Astoria" — and that hedge is the
honest thing to print. **Overturned by:** the LIC corpus producing a verdict that claims the building
with a current LIC address line; as of this wave it produces none.
