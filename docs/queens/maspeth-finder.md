# Maspeth, Queens — FINDER dossier (wave 4, Community District 5)

**Role:** FINDER. Nothing in this file is a verdict. No fact below is confirmed, adjudicated or
ranked. Everything is a CANDIDATE with its source URL attached. The verifier decides.

**Quoting convention used throughout this file:**
- Text inside `" "` following the marker **QUOTE:** is copied verbatim from the cited source.
- Text following the marker **PARAPHRASE:** is my own wording summarising the source. It is NOT a
  quote and must not be presented as one.
- Where a source's wording is itself ambiguous I say so rather than tidying it.

**Wave-4 hazard this file is written against.** Glendale, Maspeth and Ridgewood are mutually
adjacent in Queens CD5 and their borders are genuinely contested in public sources. Section 12
collects EVERY candidate place that any source assigns to a neighbourhood other than Maspeth, or
that sits on a boundary. I do not resolve any of them.

**Firewall acknowledged, not tested.** Flushing Meadows Corona Park and the eight
`Ethos.Seeds.CitiFieldPlaces` holdings are in CD4/CD7, miles east of Maspeth. Nothing in my research
reached them, and no candidate below is inside that park. The Ridgewood Savings Bank building at
107-55 Queens Boulevard (wave 3 / Forest Hills) likewise never surfaced in Maspeth research; I make
no claim on it.

**Research constraint, stated for the verifier's benefit.** The WebSearch tool budget for this
session was exhausted (200/200) before I began. All research below was done by direct HTTP fetch of
named URLs, by NYC Open Data / DCP GeoSearch API queries, and via an HTML search endpoint used as a
substitute index. Every URL cited was actually fetched by me unless explicitly flagged
"NOT FETCHED". Coverage of small businesses and restaurants is therefore thinner than usual, and I
flag that honestly in section 11 rather than padding it.

## 1. IDENTITY, NAME AND ORIGIN

### F1 — Maspeth is in Queens Community District 5, ZIP 11378
**Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maspeth,_Queens
**QUOTE:** "Maspeth is located in Queens Community District 5 and its ZIP Code is 11378. It is
patrolled by the New York City Police Department's 104th Precinct."

Corroborated independently by NYC Parks: every NYC Parks property I checked inside Maspeth returns
`Community Board: 5` and `Zip Code: 11378` (see F30–F44).

### F2 — Etymology, version A: "an inundating tidal river"
**Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maspeth,_Queens
**QUOTE:** the name derives from the Mespeatches, "one of the 13 main tribes that inhabited Long
Island," translated to mean "an inundating tidal river."

### F3 — Etymology, version B: "bad water place" or "scattered settlement"
**Source:** https://www.nycgovparks.org/parks/reiff-playground/history (NYC Parks Historical Signs
Project)
**QUOTE:** "The name of the area is a word of the Mespaechtes tribe, meaning \"bad water place,\" or
\"scattered settlement.\""

### F4 — Etymology, version C: the Mespat, at the head of Newtown Creek
**Source:** https://www.nycgovparks.org/parks/luke-j-lang-square/history
**QUOTE:** "\"Maspeth\" is derived from the name of the indigenous people, the Mespat, who inhabited
the region at the head of Newtown Creek."

**Source:** https://www.nycgovparks.org/parks/quick-brown-fox-triangle/history
**QUOTE:** "Maspeth derives its name from that of an Indian tribe, the Mespat, who inhabited the
region at the head of Newtown Creek."

### F5 — Etymology, version D: at the MOUTH of Newtown Creek (NYC Parks contradicts itself)
**Source:** https://www.nycgovparks.org/parks/hull-triangle/history
**QUOTE:** "The name Maspeth is derived from the Mespat, a Native American tribe that inhabited the
region at the mouth of Newtown Creek."

**FINDER NOTE, not a verdict:** NYC Parks' own sign texts say "head of Newtown Creek" (Lang, Quick
Brown Fox) and "mouth of Newtown Creek" (Hull). Four different etymologies (F2–F5) are in play
across three source families. The verifier should decide what, if anything, is safe to state.

### F6 — First English settlement in Queens, 1642, under Rev. Francis Doughty
**Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maspeth,_Queens
**QUOTE:** "In 1642, they settled Maspat, under a charter granted to Rev. Francis Doughty, making
Maspeth the first English settlement in Queens."
**QUOTE (same page):** "Conflicts with the Maspat tribe forced many settlers to move to what is now
Elmhurst in 1643."

### F7 — NYC Parks' version of the same founding, with a different causal account
**Source:** https://www.nycgovparks.org/parks/garlinge-triangle/history
**QUOTE:** "The first colony settled by the English in Queens County, Maspeth was home to the Mespat
Indians. In 1642, Reverend Francis Doughty from the Massachusetts colony was expelled from the
colony after the Mohicans and Matinecocks burned down his settlement in retaliation for the massacre
of Indians in Connecticut. Settlers then moved inland to what is now Elmhurst."

### F8 — NYC Parks' third version, told from the Indigenous side
**Source:** https://www.nycgovparks.org/parks/frank-principe-park/history
**QUOTE:** "This land was home to the Mespeatches until the Dutch purchased it in 1635 and issued a
land grant to English settlers to establish Maspeth in 1642. The Indigenous population thought the
transaction was akin to leasing or sharing the land. The idea of owning land in the Western sense
was a foreign concept, which led to violent conflicts. The English settlers ultimately moved inland
to what became Elmhurst."

**FINDER NOTE:** F6, F7 and F8 agree on 1642 + Doughty + retreat to Elmhurst, and disagree on why.
The Frank Principe text is the most recent NYC Parks sign and the only one that centres the
Mespeatches. Recorded, not ranked.

### F9 — Resettlement 1652; the village of Middleburgh
**Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maspeth,_Queens
**PARAPHRASE:** Resettlement occurred in 1652 with 28 English Quakers, and the community developed
water and milling industries along Newtown Creek and Maspeth Creek. The article's own words for the
industries are "sizable water and milling industries along Newtown Creek and Maspeth Creek."

**Source:** https://www.nycgovparks.org/parks/reiff-playground/history
**QUOTE:** "Although a Native American attack destroyed the town the year after settlement, settlers
returned in 1652 and established the village of Middleburgh."

### F10 — The Newtown Patent
**Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maspeth,_Queens
**PARAPHRASE:** The article states the "Newtown Patent" granted 13,000 acres to settlers through the
first deed signed on Long Island.

### F11 — Columbusville and Laurel Hill: absorbed sub-names
**Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maspeth,_Queens
**QUOTE:** Columbusville, developed by Edward Dunn in 1854–55, "was subsequently absorbed into
Maspeth. The name fell into disuse in the 1890s."
**PARAPHRASE:** The same article states that Laurel Hill was the post-colonial era name for what
became West Maspeth, commemorated by Laurel Hill Boulevard (56th Road).

### F12 — Melvina: a lost sub-name
**Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maspeth,_Queens
**QUOTE:** "Melvina Place, off Maspeth Avenue, is named after a small section of Maspeth, which was
to the east of the approximate intersection of Maspeth Plank Road and Rust Street."

### F17 — "Surrounded on three sides by cemeteries" — a claim NYC Parks repeats three times
**Source:** https://www.nycgovparks.org/parks/luke-j-lang-square/history
**QUOTE:** "Maspeth is surrounded on three sides by cemeteries, including the Mt. Olivet Lutheran,
Calvary, Linden Hill, New Calvary, and Mt. Zion cemeteries."

**Source:** https://www.nycgovparks.org/parks/quick-brown-fox-triangle/history
**QUOTE:** "The neighborhood is in the middle of Queens, and is surrounded on three sides by
cemeteries: Mt. Olivet Lutheran Cemetery, Calvary Cemetery, Linden Hill Cemetery, New Calvary
Cemetery, and Mt. Zion Cemetery."

**Source:** https://www.nycgovparks.org/parks/hull-triangle/history
**QUOTE:** "The neighborhood is located in central Queens, and is surrounded on three sides by
cemeteries: the Mount Olivet Lutheran Cemetery, the Calvary Cemetery, the Linden Hill Cemetery, the
New Calvary Cemetery, and the Mount Zion Cemetery."

**FINDER NOTE — IMPORTANT.** NYC Parks calls it "Mt. Olivet **Lutheran** Cemetery" in all three
signs. Mount Olivet Cemetery in Maspeth is a **non-sectarian** cemetery; the Lutheran cemetery in
this district is **All Faiths Cemetery (formerly Lutheran Cemetery) in Middle Village**. NYC Parks
appears to have conflated two cemeteries in a sign text it reused three times. Do not repeat "Mt.
Olivet Lutheran" without verification. Also note: of the five cemeteries named, at most two are
inside Maspeth — see section 5 and section 12.

**Source:**

is the *absence*: the campaigns that failed, and what survives without protection. Section 4 is
where that story lives. **This is a structural feature of Maspeth, not a research failure.**

## 4. ST. SAVIOUR'S CHURCH — the richest single subject in the dossier

The church is GONE from Maspeth. Its salvaged fabric sits in Middle Village. Both facts matter for
the §12 assignment question.

### F23 — What it was
**Source:** https://nylandmarks.org/news/revisiting-an-unusual-project-dismantling-an-1847-upjohn-church/
(New York Landmarks Conservancy — institutional source, the organisation that documented the
dismantling)
**QUOTE:** "St. Saviour's, a Carpenter Gothic church by Richard Upjohn, stood for more than 160 years
in Maspeth, Queens."

**Source:** https://qns.com/2025/12/st-saviours-church-maspeth-pieces-middle-village/
**QUOTE:** "St. Saviour's was founded in 1848 on land donated by Rep. James Maurice, an iconic Queens
politician of the 19th century. Famed New York Architect Richard Upjohn designed the church in the
Carpenter Gothic style based on unused designs for a church in Manhattan."

**Source:** same QNS article, editor's note appended to the piece
**QUOTE:** "An earlier version incorrectly implied that the church was hit with artillery fire during
the American Revolution. This was in reference to the historical site's use during the war, but the
church itself was built in 1847, and founded in 1848. We regret the error and any confusion which
may have resulted."
**FINDER NOTE:** Built 1847, founded 1848 — the source itself distinguishes these and corrects a
prior error. Wikipedia's Maspeth article separately says the church was "built in 1847 at Rust Street
and 57th Drive." QNS gives the location as "off the corner of Rust Street and **57th Road**." The
street suffix is inconsistent between sources (Drive vs. Road). Do not pick one silently.

### F24 — Original address
**Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maspeth,_Queens
**QUOTE:** St. Saviour's Church, "built in 1847 at Rust Street and 57th Drive, was located on land
formerly owned by lawyer and politician James Maurice."

**Source:** https://nylandmarks.org/news/revisiting-an-unusual-project-dismantling-an-1847-upjohn-church/
**QUOTE:** "The historic framing of the church has remained in two trailers in All Faiths Cemetery in

**QUOTE (photo caption):** "The wood framing of St. Saviour's Church stored in locked trailers at All

**Source:** https://qns.com/2025/12/st-saviours-church-maspeth-pieces-middle-village/
**QUOTE:** "Here lies in pieces the remains of St. Saviour's Church, a Maspeth landmark that has been

## 16A. QUOTE-INTEGRITY PASS (what I did to stop myself fabricating quotes)

The brief warns that a previous finder presented four paraphrases as verbatim quotes, one of which
inverted a source's meaning. Two things in my method created that exact risk, so I want them on the
record:

1. **My first fetch of the Wikipedia Maspeth article went through a summarising fetch tool**, which
   returns a small model's rendering of the page — not the page. Anything I had marked **QUOTE:** from
   that fetch was, at that moment, a quote I had not actually seen in the source.
2. **I therefore re-fetched the raw article** and string-matched fifteen of the highest-risk quoted
   passages against the real text. **Fourteen matched exactly. One ("Hagstrom Map, the best-selling
   brand of road maps…") failed the match**, which I chased down: the raw text renders it as "Hagstrom
   Map , the best-selling…" with a space before the comma, an artifact of wiki-link markup. The quote
   is sound; the match failed for a formatting reason.

**Residual risk the verifier should know about.** The same summarising-fetch caveat applies to the
material I drew from the **Mount Olivet Cemetery**, **NRHP Queens listings**, and **Newtown Creek**
articles, which I did NOT re-fetch raw. Passages attributed to those three pages are marked
**PARAPHRASE** wherever I was not certain of the wording — but treat any **QUOTE:** from those three
as needing a raw re-check before it is printed. Everything sourced to NYC Parks, NYC Open Data,
