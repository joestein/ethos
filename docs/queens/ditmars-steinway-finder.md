## THE BOUNDARY PROBLEM — read this first

This is the central hazard of this wave and it is worse than "sources disagree at the edges."
The core difficulty:

- **"Ditmars-Steinway" as a single hyphenated name is largely a real-estate and MTA-adjacent
  coinage, not a unit that most sources treat as primary.** Sources overwhelmingly describe
  *Ditmars* and *Steinway* as two separate **sub-sections of Astoria**, not as a neighborhood
  coordinate with Astoria.
- Consequently **almost every notable place in "Ditmars-Steinway" is described by its own source as
  being in "Astoria."** A rule of "if the source says Astoria, it isn't ours" would empty this
  neighborhood entirely. A rule of "Astoria means Ditmars-Steinway when it's north of X" is an
  adjudication I am not permitted to make.
- The three neighborhoods in this wave (Astoria, Ditmars-Steinway, Long Island City) are not
  parallel entities in the sources: two of them are arguably containers for the third.

**F-B1.** Ditmars boundaries. Source: Wikipedia, *Ditmars, Queens*.
> "Ditmars is a middle class section of Astoria bounded by Bowery Bay to the north, 31st Street and
> the Steinway subsection to the east, 23rd Avenue to the south, and the East River to the west."
Note the framing: "a middle class **section of Astoria**."
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ditmars,_Queens

## CANDIDATE FACTS

### History and naming

**F1.** Ditmars is named for Ditmars Boulevard, "which was named in honor of Abram Ditmars, the
first mayor of Long Island City, New York, elected in 1870."
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ditmars,_Queens

**F2.** Ditmars Boulevard article gives Abram Ditmars as "the first Mayor of Long Island City in
1870" and says he was the son of Dr. Dow Ditmars, a community physician; the street is named after
the Ditmars family (paraphrase of the extraction's framing). **Date conflict to check: F1 says
"elected in 1870," F2 says "Mayor ... in 1870."**
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ditmars_Boulevard

**F3.** Ditmars Boulevard is 3.9 miles (6.3 km), "divided into two segments; one travels through the
neighborhood of Ditmars, located within Astoria, and the other acts as a service road for the Grand
Central Parkway near LaGuardia Airport in East Elmhurst." Runs from Shore Boulevard near the East
River to Astoria Boulevard and 111th Street in East Elmhurst; the Grand Central Parkway bisects it.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ditmars_Boulevard

**F4.** Astoria's name: Stephen Halsey, "known as the father of Astoria," expanded the settlement in
the 1830s; in 1839 he sought funding for an Episcopal Female Seminary and "hoped to secure a
donation of $2000" from John Jacob Astor for naming rights. Astor "invested only $500, but the name
stayed nonetheless." (Background context for the parent neighborhood.)
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astoria,_Queens

**F5.** Old Astoria / Astoria Village began in the early 19th century when "affluent New Yorkers
constructed large residences along the Hallett's Cove shoreline, in the vicinity of 12th and 14th
Streets." (Not Ditmars-Steinway — context only.)
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astoria,_Queens

### Steinway Village — the company town (richest vein)

**F6.** "Steinway Village was built as its own town, and included a new factory (still used today)
with its own foundry and sawmill, houses for employees, kindergarten, lending library, post office,
volunteer fire department, and parks." Developed through 1870–80 as William Steinway purchased
property.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steinway_%26_Sons

**F7.** From the Astoria article: in 1870 "Steinway and Sons piano makers ... built a piano factory
and worker housing in the northernmost portion of Astoria." The "Steinway company town provided a
range of services including bilingual schools for workers' children; it also included a sawmill and
foundry, as well as a streetcar line."
**Note the tension with F6:** F6 says "kindergarten, lending library, post office, volunteer fire
department"; F7 says "bilingual schools" and "a streetcar line." Overlapping but non-identical lists.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astoria,_Queens

**F8.** "Steinway Street, one of the major streets in the Astoria and Long Island City neighborhoods
of Queens, is named after the company."
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steinway_%26_Sons

**F9.** Steinway & Sons headquarters/factory: "One Steinway Place, Queens, New York, New York, U.S."
(Wikipedia) / "One Steinway Place, Astoria, NY 11105 - (718) 721-2600" (company site).
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steinway_%26_Sons | https://www.steinway.com/about/factory-tour

**F10.** INSTITUTIONAL SOURCE. Steinway & Sons' own site: "Steinways are still handmade in the
Steinway New York and Hamburg factories, as they have been since 1880."
https://www.steinway.com/about/factory-tour

**F11.** INSTITUTIONAL SOURCE, and a visitor-facing fact that has changed: "We are no longer

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astoria,_Queens

**F13.** Steinway Tunnel: "A pair of tubes carrying the IRT Flushing Line (7 and <7> trains) of the
New York City Subway under the East River." In July 1891 William Steinway became a major funder and
shareholder and "became the new chairman of the company, so the tunnel was named after him." Work
began June 7, 1892; halted after a December 1892 explosion; IRT resumed in 1905, tubes complete
September 1907; line opened June 13, 1915.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steinway_Tunnel

F35–F42: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astoria_Pool (redirects into the Astoria Park article)
