## Method and its limits (read before using this file)

1. **WebSearch was unavailable for this pass.** The session's search budget (200/200) was already
   spent by sibling agents before this agent's first call. All material below came from
   **WebFetch against URLs guessed directly** from institutional/primary hosts. That means coverage
   is skewed toward things with a predictable URL (Wikipedia, NRHP/LPC list pages, agency pages) and
   away from anything that needs discovery. **Absence of a topic below is not evidence of absence.**
2. **WebFetch returns a small-model summary of the page, not the raw page.** So text presented here
   inside quotation marks is *the summarizer's reproduction* of the source's wording. It is
   materially more reliable than my paraphrase, but it is still second-hand. Items where the
   wording carries the claim are tagged `[QUOTE-VIA-SUMMARIZER]`. Items where no quoted string came
   back and I am restating the summarizer's prose are tagged `[PARAPHRASE]`. **The verifier should
   re-pull the raw URL for anything load-bearing.**
3. `nycgovparks.org` returned **HTTP 403** on every attempt (`/parks/rockaway-beach-and-boardwalk`,
   `/parks/Q003/`). `edc.nyc` returned **403**. So the city Parks and EDC primary sources are
   *unread*. Park facts below are second-hand via Wikipedia and are the weakest material here.
4. **No restaurant or business candidates were sourced at all.** Not one. There is therefore no
   DOHMH/DCWP-only business in this dossier — the category is empty, not thin. Dining/retail is a
   flat gap for the next pass.

Counts: **67 candidate facts** (numbered F1–F68; F48 is a negative result — an NPS 404 — and carries
no candidate content). **31 candidate places** (P1–P31): 17 in-scope candidates for this page
(P1–P17), 5 contested (P18–P22), and 9 recorded expressly to keep them *off* this page (P23–P31).

## G. Transit

**F59.** **Far Rockaway–Mott Avenue** — Mott Avenue & Beach 22nd Street; A train eastern terminal,
served "at all times"; original station opened **July 29, 1869** as an LIRR Far Rockaway Branch
station; LIRR ran it until 1950, when a fire damaged the Jamaica Bay viaduct; the city acquired
trackage west of Mott Avenue **June 11, 1952**; A train service began June 1956, with "a new Far
Rockaway subway station...opening on January 16, 1958"; elevated on a concrete viaduct, two tracks
and one island platform; a **$117 million** renovation ran 2009–2012 adding "metallic facades and a
dome enclosure," elevators and ADA access, reopening **May 11, 2012**; MTA Arts for Transit glass
work titled ***Respite***; 2024 ridership **961,694**, up 12.8%, ranking 302 of 423 stations.
