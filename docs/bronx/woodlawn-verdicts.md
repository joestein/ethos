## C. Boundary rulings

The finder raised two boundary facts and asked the verifier to rule on them.
Both are adjudicated here, per the dispatch's instruction that boundary
findings be ruled on explicitly.

**Ruling 1 — the Woodlawn subway station belongs to Norwood, not Woodlawn.**
Confirmed on two independent Wikipedia articles, re-fetched directly (B1, B3
above). Norwood is community district 7 in the roster and is `in_scope:
false`. **This is not a two-page collision** — no Norwood page exists in this
corpus to claim the station, so there is no risk of the same landmark
appearing on two shipped pages. The correct handling, which the finder
already applied, is: name the station in Woodlawn's "Getting there" section
only as the honest negative ("no subway serves Woodlawn directly; the nearest
station, confusingly named Woodlawn, is actually in Norwood") — Case B under
the proximity rule, since the source affirmatively places it in a different,
named neighborhood. Do not present it as Woodlawn's station.

**Ruling 2 — the Aisling Irish Community and Cultural Center is outside New
York City entirely and must not appear as a Woodlawn place.** Re-fetched
directly: `aislingcenter.org` gives its own address as "990 McLean Ave.
Yonkers, NY 10704." This is not a neighboring-roster-row collision either
(unlike City Island Bridge vs. Pelham Bay Park) — it is a business whose own
site places it outside NYC and outside the state's five boroughs entirely, in
Westchester County. No shipped Bronx page should claim it, and none should
"lose" it to another Bronx page either, since it does not belong to the Bronx
at all. The finder's exclusion is correct and is affirmed. It may be
mentioned in prose (as the neighborhood's own Wikipedia article does, calling
it a McLean Avenue institution) only if the prose is explicit that the
address itself is in Yonkers — never presented as a Woodlawn (Bronx) place
record.
