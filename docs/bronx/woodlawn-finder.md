## Neighborhood-level history and orientation

- Wikipedia's article title for this neighborhood is "Woodlawn Heights,
  Bronx," redirected from "Woodlawn, Bronx." Opening sentence: "Woodlawn
  Heights, also known as Woodlawn, is a predominantly Irish-American working
  class neighborhood at the very north end of the borough of the Bronx in
  New York City." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodlawn_Heights,_Bronx
- Boundaries: "bounded by McLean Avenue to the north (slightly north of New
  York City's border with the city of Yonkers in Westchester County), the
  Bronx River to the east, Woodlawn Cemetery to the south, and Van Cortlandt
  Park to the west." — same source
- Neighborhood area: 0.240 square miles. — same source
- 2020 Census population: 7,985. — same source
- "Historically a German neighborhood," the article states it is "now a
  mostly Irish American neighborhood," and separately that it "remains one
  of the few communities to which young and newly arrived Irish immigrants
  regularly arrive en masse." — same source
- Community District: Bronx Community Board 12. — same source
- ZIP code: 10470. Area codes: 718, 347, 929, 917. — same source
- Katonah Avenue "runs north–south through the heart of Woodlawn Heights"
  and is "a popular destination for its many Irish pubs and restaurants,"
  with named specialty businesses limited to "The Emerald Isle Immigration
  Center, an Italian bakery, an Italian restaurant, an Irish butcher shop"
  (the latter three not given proper names in the article). — same source
- McLean Avenue (running east–west) is separately described as home to
  Irish pubs, restaurants and gift shops, with the article noting this
  strip is "technically located in Yonkers." — same source
- Police: 47th Precinct, NYPD, located at 4111 Laconia Avenue, which also
  patrols neighboring Williamsbridge. — same source
- Fire service: FDNY Engine Co. 63/Ladder Co. 39/Battalion 15, located at
  755 East 233rd Street. — same source
- Oneida Triangle is "a memorial to residents of Woodlawn Heights who served
  in World War I." No size, exact address, or dedication date given. —
  same source
- Muskrat Cove is "the northernmost segment of the Bronx River Greenway,"
  located between "the Woodlawn station and the southbound off-ramp from
  the Bronx River Parkway to East 233rd Street." No size or establishment
  date given. — same source
- PS 19, "Judith K. Weiss School" (also called "Weiss Woodlawn School"),
  serves the neighborhood. No address or founding date given in this
  article. — same source

## Getting there

**Subway.** No subway line serves Woodlawn directly. Wikipedia states this
explicitly: "There are currently no New York City Subway routes serving
Woodlawn; the Woodlawn station on the IRT Jerome Avenue Line is actually in
Norwood." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodlawn_Heights,_Bronx

- That station (4 train, all times) is the northern terminal of the IRT
  Jerome Avenue Line, at Bainbridge Avenue & Jerome Avenue, opened April 15,
  1918, designed by IRT chief architect Squire J. Vickers, and listed on the
  National Register of Historic Places (July 6, 2005). It is described as
  sitting "outside Woodlawn Cemetery," with the cemetery's main entrance "a
  block north" — but the intersection itself "is in the Norwood
  neighborhood of the Bronx, and not in Woodlawn." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodlawn_(IRT_Jerome_Avenue_Line)
- The station is not currently ADA-accessible; the MTA announced in July
  2025 plans to add elevators as part of its 2025–2029 capital program. —
  same source
- The Bx95 route is a subway shuttle bus running between the Woodlawn
  subway station and the Kingsbridge Road station along Jerome Avenue,
  used "to temporarily replace portions of service on the New York City
  Subway due to system maintenance." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_bus_routes_in_the_Bronx

**Metro-North.** Woodlawn station, on Metro-North's Harlem Line, serves "the
Woodlawn section of the Bronx," at 555 East 233rd Street. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodlawn_(Metro-North_station)

- The neighborhood's own article confirms: "commuter rail service does
  exist in Woodlawn station of the Metro-North Railroad's Harlem Line,
  providing service to Westchester County and Putnam County as well as
  Manhattan." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodlawn_Heights,_Bronx
- The station has two high-level side platforms, each accommodating four
  cars, across four total tracks. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodlawn_(Metro-North_station)
- Metered and private parking is available, accessed via Webster Avenue. —
  same source
- Connecting NYC Transit bus routes at the station: Bx16 and Bx31. — same
  source
- The line here was originally developed by the New York and Harlem
  Railroad in the mid-1840s, rebuilt during a late-19th-century grade
  elimination project, and transferred to Metro-North operation in 1983;
  until the 1990s it served as the changeover point between catenary and
  third-rail power systems. — same source
- As of June 2025, the MTA began adding elevators here as part of a wider
  Bronx-stations accessibility upgrade. — same source

**Bus.** The neighborhood article states: "Woodlawn Heights is served by
the Bx16, Bx31, Bx34 and BxM4 buses operated by MTA Regional Bus
Operations." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodlawn_Heights,_Bronx

- **Bx16**: runs from "Norwood" at East 206th Street and Bainbridge Avenue
  to "Eastchester" at Ropes Avenue and Boston Road, via Bainbridge Avenue,
  East 233rd Street, and Nereid Avenue. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_bus_routes_in_the_Bronx
- **Bx31**: runs from "Westchester Square" at Lane Avenue and East Tremont
  Avenue to "Woodlawn" at Katonah Avenue and East 242nd Street, via
  Eastchester Road and East 233rd Street. — same source
- **Bx34**: runs from "Fordham" at Valentine Avenue and Fordham Road to
  "Woodlawn" at Katonah Avenue and East 242nd Street, via Bainbridge Avenue
  and East 233rd Street; some trips terminate at Jerome Avenue. — same
  source
- **BxM4** (express): terminates in the Bronx at "Katonah Avenue and 242nd
  Street" in Woodlawn; in Manhattan it runs along Fifth Avenue (southbound)
  and Madison Avenue (northbound), crossing via the Madison Avenue Bridge
  and continuing via the Grand Concourse and Jerome Avenue, with its
  Manhattan terminus at Fifth Avenue/27th Street (drop-off) and Madison
  Avenue/29th Street (pick-up), in Midtown. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BxM4_(New_York_City_bus)

## Places

### 1. Woodlawn Cemetery

**What it is:** A National Historic Landmark rural cemetery founded in 1863,
occupying the neighborhood's entire southern boundary. `searched: complete`

- Address given by the cemetery's own site: 4199 Webster Ave, Bronx, New
  York 10470. Wikipedia separately gives the location as "Webster Avenue
  and East 233rd Street, Woodlawn, Bronx." — https://woodlawn.org/ and
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodlawn_Cemetery_(Bronx)
- Founded 1863, during the Civil War. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodlawn_Cemetery_(Bronx)
  and independently, the cemetery's own site: "New York's World Class
  Cemetery Since 1863." — https://woodlawn.org/
- Over 400 acres (160 hectares); resting place for "more than 300,000
  people." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodlawn_Cemetery_(Bronx)
- Originally sited in undeveloped Yonkers, Westchester County; the land was
  annexed to New York City in 1874. — same source
- Designated a National Historic Landmark, listed on the National Register
  of Historic Places, and listed on the New York State Register of Historic
  Places, all on the same date: June 23, 2011. — same source
- Within the cemetery, the gravesite of Admiral David Glasgow Farragut
  received its own, separate individual National Register listing on
  October 16, 2012 (reference #12001008), at "Lot 1429–44, Section 14,
  Aurora Hill Plot." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places_listings_in_the_Bronx
- The cemetery's own site independently calls this a "landmark within a
  landmark," describing the Farragut gravesite's separate NHL status as
  recognizing the cemetery's significance "from the Industrial Age
  forward." — https://woodlawn.org/
- Architects and designers with work in the cemetery include McKim, Mead &
  White; John Russell Pope; James Gamble Rogers; Cass Gilbert; Carrère and
  Hastings; and Sir Edwin Lutyens. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodlawn_Cemetery_(Bronx)
- Landscape architects with work there include Beatrix Jones Farrand
  (Harkness Memorial Garden and the Milliken Memorial) and Ellen Biddle
  Shipman (landscapes for three mausoleums); the Olmsted Brothers firm
  handled lot designs for the Frederick Constable and Ernest Stauffen
  family plots. — https://www.nps.gov/places/woodlawn-cemetery.htm
- The cemetery contains "nearly 1,300 private lots, many designed by some
  of the most notable American architects," set within "a serious
  collection of trees." — same source
- The cemetery's own site separately claims it holds the "largest and
  finest collection of funerary art in the country" and states it holds
  Level II Accredited Arboretum status; **flagged, not carried as an
  independently-confirmed superlative**, since a cemetery's own site
  describing its own collection is a self-description, per the finder
  contract's rule on an operator's own claims about itself. —
  https://woodlawn.org/
- Seven Commonwealth War Graves from the First and Second World Wars are
  located in the cemetery. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodlawn_Cemetery_(Bronx)
- Eight "Great Trees" within the cemetery have been individually designated
  by NYC Parks, in rounds dated 1985 and 2024. — same source
- A stone plaque within the grounds marks the site of a 1776 Continental
  Army redoubt used during a Revolutionary War retreat. — same source
- The cemetery is nicknamed "Brewer's Row" in one section for its
  concentration of burials from historic brewing families. — same source
- The cemetery's own site states current visiting hours as 8:30 AM to 4:30
  PM daily, and gives contact information: (718) 920-0500,
  info@woodlawn.org. This is cited as the cemetery's own description of its
  own operations, not as independent confirmation of current status. —
  https://woodlawn.org/
- Notable people the cemetery's own site names as buried there: Celia Cruz,
  Miles Davis, Elizabeth Cady Stanton, Joseph Pulitzer, Nellie Bly, and
  Irving Berlin. — https://woodlawn.org/
- The main Wikipedia article separately names, among others buried there:
  Charles Evans Hughes (Chief Justice), Robert Moses, Vito Marcantonio,
  Cicely Tyson, Harry Carey, George M. Cohan, Duke Ellington, Otto
  Preminger, W.C. Handy, Fritz Kreisler, King Oliver, Max Roach, Ace
  Frehley, Herman Melville, Dorothy Parker, Countee Cullen, Damon Runyon,
  E.L. Doctorow, Gertrude Ederle, Harriet Quimby, Grantland Rice, Bat
  Masterson, F.W. Woolworth, Rowland Hussey Macy, Madam C.J. Walker,
  Archibald Gracie, and Mihajlo Pupin. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodlawn_Cemetery_(Bronx)
- Jay Gould, financier, "was interred in the Gould family mausoleum at
  Woodlawn Cemetery, The Bronx, New York." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Gould
- Isidor Straus (co-owner of Macy's, died on the Titanic) "was first buried
  in the Straus-Kohns Mausoleum at Beth-El Cemetery in Brooklyn, and he was
  then moved to the Straus Mausoleum in Woodlawn Cemetery in the Bronx in
  1928." His wife Ida's body was never recovered; "the family collected
  water from the wreck site and placed it in an urn in the mausoleum," and
  the couple "are memorialized on a cenotaph outside the mausoleum with a
  quote from the Song of Solomon." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isidor_Straus
