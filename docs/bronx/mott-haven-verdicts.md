## Flagged items — explicit rulings

**1. Bronx General Post Office: 1975 vs. 1976 exterior designation date.**
Resolved, not merely adjudicated as a conflict. The Wikipedia article on the building explains it in its own footnote: November 1975 is when a newspaper (*Staten Island Advance*) reported on the pending designation; the LPC's own designation report — the actual act of designation — is dated **September 14, 1976**, which matches the boroughwide landmarks list exactly. Ruling: the November 25, 1975 date is **refuted** as the designation date; **September 14, 1976 is the correct exterior designation date** and is what should ship if this fact is published.

**2. Mott Haven Library's 1969 date vs. the historic district's 1969 date.**
These are the same event, not two designations. The boroughwide landmarks list has no standalone "Mott Haven Library" entry — the Bronx's only individually-landmarked NYPL branches are Morrisania, Hunts Point, and Woodstock (plus the unrelated Van Schaick/Huntington reading room). The library sits inside the Mott Haven Historic District, designated July 29, 1969, and that is almost certainly what the library's own site means by "declared a landmark in 1969." Ruling: publish the library's landmark status as inherited from the historic district's July 29, 1969 designation, not as an independent individual landmarking of the library building.

**3. The 149th Street transit cluster at the neighborhood's boundary.**
The finder's own Case C flag is correct and should stand as written: the Wikipedia article associates 149th Street–Grand Concourse and Third Avenue–149th Street with Mott Haven but never states whether the physical station entrances sit north or south of East 149th Street, which is the neighborhood's own stated boundary line. No independent source was found in this pass that resolves the station's exact side of the line. Ruling: **uncertain on location** — per site-builder.md §8's Case C rule, if a "Getting there" section is authored, name the stations and their lines without asserting they are "in" or "on the edge of" Mott Haven, and record the gap for a later pass. Do not silently upgrade to Case A.

**4. Exclusion of the Bronx Opera House Hotel.**
The exclusion was correct. Re-fetched https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronx_Opera_House directly: the infobox states the building's neighborhood as "Melrose neighborhood of the Bronx, New York City," in plain terms, not merely by inference. The address (436 East 149th Street) sits on Mott Haven's own stated northern boundary street, which makes this exactly the kind of near-miss site-builder.md's rule 8 warns about (the Southbury/Woodbury mislocation precedent) — geographic proximity to a boundary does not override an explicit, sourced neighborhood assignment. Ruling: **exclusion upheld**. One additional note for whoever revisits this candidate under Melrose: the finder's "opened August 11, 2013 per the hotel's own site" attribution does not hold up — re-fetching https://www.operahousehotel.com/ found no opening date anywhere on that page; the August 11, 2013 date appears only in the Wikipedia article on the Opera House building itself, not on the hotel's own site as cited. Not a live issue for Mott Haven since the place is excluded, but worth flagging so a Melrose finder doesn't inherit a bad citation.
