## Neighborhood-level history and orientation

- The Wikipedia article "Fordham, Bronx" states the neighborhood is "roughly bordered by East 196th Street to the north, the Harlem River to the west, Fordham Road to the south, and Southern Boulevard to the east." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham,_Bronx
- The same article states the neighborhood spans two community districts: "Community District 5, south of Fordham Road" and "Community District 7, north of Fordham Road." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham,_Bronx
- History: Dutch settler Jan Archer "established a community at what is now 225th Street near the Harlem River" in 1666; from 1671–1762 "Fordham Manor" operated as a roughly six-square-mile patent. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham,_Bronx
- A manor house was built on Rose Hill in 1751; Andrew Carrigan purchased the Rose Hill estate in 1838–1841; Bishop John Joseph Hughes bought it for $29,750 and founded St. Joseph's Seminary (1840) and St. Joseph's College (1841, later Fordham University), transferring the college to the Jesuits in 1846 for $40,000. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham,_Bronx
- "Old Fordham Village remained rural until about 1900, when New York City began to expand." Fordham's name "may have been a reference to its location near a shallow crossing ('ford') of the Bronx River, or may have been a reference to John Fordham, a fifteenth-century English priest." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham,_Bronx
- From the 1920s through the 1970s Fordham "was a predominantly middle class White (of Irish and Jewish origins) neighborhood" until "many families moved to the suburbs"; it is "now predominantly Latino American and African American" with "a significant historic Italian and Albanian population." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham,_Bronx
- Population "around 43,394 people" (2011 figure) across 0.507 sq mi; median income $26,143 (2011). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham,_Bronx
- A January 9, 2022 fire in the neighborhood killed "at least 19 people, including nine children." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham,_Bronx
- ZIP codes given for the neighborhood: 10453, 10457, 10458, and 10468. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham,_Bronx

## Places

### 1. Fordham University — Rose Hill campus

**What it is:** The Jesuit university's original campus, founding institution of the wider Fordham neighborhood's name and history. `searched: complete`

- Founded 1841 by Bishop John Hughes; Hughes purchased "the 106-acre Rose Hill Manor farm in the village of Fordham, New York, for $29,750" in 1839; the campus now occupies 85 acres (34.4 ha). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_University
- The "Rose Hill" name was "originally given to the site in 1787 by its owner, Robert Watts, a wealthy New York merchant, in honor of his family's ancestral home in Scotland." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_University
- Hughes founded St. Joseph's Seminary (1840) and St. Joseph's College (1841, later Fordham University) on the site, transferring the college to the Jesuits in 1846 for $40,000. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham,_Bronx
- The campus features "Collegiate Gothic architecture, expansive lawns, ivy-covered buildings, and cobblestone streets," and sits "just north of the Belmont neighborhood" and "immediately west of the Bronx Zoo and the New York Botanical Garden." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_University
- Includes Fordham College at Rose Hill and the Gabelli School of Business among the schools housed there. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_University

**Cunniffe House ("Rose Hill," Fordham University Administration Building):**
- "The administration building at Rose Hill, constructed in 1838 and one of the oldest buildings on campus." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_University
- Listed as "Rose Hill (Fordham University Administration Building)," designated a NYC landmark August 18, 1970, per the boroughwide landmarks list. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_New_York_City_Designated_Landmarks_in_the_Bronx
- No dedicated Wikipedia article was found under "Rose Hill (Fordham University)" (404) — the above two sources are the only material located in this pass.

**University Church:**
- Built 1845 as a seminary chapel; contains the old altar from St. Patrick's Cathedral and stained glass from King Louis Philippe I; its bell is named "old Edgar" and, per the article, "may have been the inspiration for [Poe's] poem 'The Bells.'" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_University

**St. John's Residence Hall:**
- Address "Fordham Road and East 179th Street"; designated a NYC landmark August 18, 1970, per the boroughwide landmarks list. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_New_York_City_Designated_Landmarks_in_the_Bronx
- No independent Wikipedia article was found for this building beyond the landmarks-list entry.

**Alumni House:**
- Address "East Fordham Road and 191st Street"; designated a NYC landmark February 3, 1981, per the boroughwide landmarks list. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_New_York_City_Designated_Landmarks_in_the_Bronx
- No independent Wikipedia article was found for this building beyond the landmarks-list entry.

### 2. Edgar Allan Poe Cottage

**What it is:** The wood-frame cottage where Poe lived and wrote in his final years, now a museum. `searched: complete`, but see the boundary flag above on whether it sits in Fordham Heights or Fordham Manor — not resolved here.

- Address given as 2640 Grand Concourse, Fordham, Bronx, NY. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Allan_Poe_Cottage
- **Construction-date conflict, not resolved here:** the cottage is dated "built in 1797" by one part of its own Wikipedia article and "1812" elsewhere in the same article.
- Poe lived there with his wife Virginia Clemm and her mother Maria from around May 1846 until Virginia's death January 30, 1847. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Allan_Poe_Cottage
- Works written there per the article: the poems "Annabel Lee" and "Ulalume," the series "The Literati of New York City," and the short story "Landor's Cottage" (the article says this last was "likely inspired by" the home, a hedged attribution, not a flat claim). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Allan_Poe_Cottage
- Designated a Bronx landmark in 1962; "recognized by the New York City Landmarks Preservation Commission" February 15, 1966 — independently corroborated by the boroughwide landmarks list's "Poe Cottage" entry, same February 15, 1966 date. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Allan_Poe_Cottage and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_New_York_City_Designated_Landmarks_in_the_Bronx
- Added to the National Register of Historic Places August 19, 1980, reference #80002588, address given there as "2640 Grand Concourse" — independently corroborated by the separate National Register of Historic Places listings in the Bronx article, same date and reference number. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Allan_Poe_Cottage and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places_listings_in_the_Bronx
- "Administered by the Bronx County Historical Society since 1975"; part of the Historic House Trust; a visitor center opened in 2008. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Allan_Poe_Cottage
- Sits "in the northern part of Poe Park," near the intersection of Kingsbridge Road and Grand Concourse; the cottage was relocated to this site November 13, 1913 from its original location on Kingsbridge Road. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Allan_Poe_Cottage

### 4. Grand Concourse (boulevard) / Grand Concourse Historic District

**What it is:** The Art Deco boulevard running the length of the west-central Bronx; a National Register historic district covers part of it. `searched: complete`

- "5.2-mile-long (8.4 km) thoroughfare," 180 feet (55 m) wide for most of its length, running from East 138th Street (south) to Mosholu Parkway (north), passing through "Bedford Park, Concourse, Highbridge, Fordham, Mott Haven, Norwood and Tremont." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Concourse_(Bronx)
- Designed by Louis Aloys Risse, "an immigrant from Saint-Avold, Lorraine, France," who "first conceived of the road in 1890"; built between 1894 and 1909, with an additional extension in 1927; total cost $14 million. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Concourse_(Bronx)
- "By the mid-1930s, almost three hundred apartment buildings had been constructed" along it, featuring "Art Deco and Art Moderne architecture"; in 1939 it "was called 'the Park Avenue of middle-class Bronx residents.'" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Concourse_(Bronx)
- The Grand Concourse Historic District was "added to NRHP August 24, 1987." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Concourse_(Bronx)
- Primary architectural styles named for the corridor: "Late 19th and 20th Century Revivals, Art Deco." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Concourse_(Bronx)
- In 2011 the NYC Landmarks Preservation Commission designated numerous buildings; per the same source, the 2011 NYCL historic-district designation specifically covers the area "from 153rd to 167th Streets" — well south of Fordham Road, so this particular 2011 landmark district does not itself reach the Fordham Heights/Fordham Road stretch of the boulevard. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Concourse_(Bronx)
- At Fordham Road, "the main road passes underneath in a grade-separated junction, while the service roads intersect with Fordham Road." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Concourse_(Bronx)

### 6. Fordham Plaza

- Sits "directly above the Metro-North's Fordham station, one of the busiest in that system"; "the closest subway stop to the plaza is the Fordham Road station of the IND Concourse Line six blocks west." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_Plaza

### 8. Fordham Road (shopping corridor / Business Improvement District)

**What it is:** The Bronx's major commercial spine, run as a managed retail district. `searched: complete`

- "A major thoroughfare in the Bronx... that runs west-east from the Harlem River to Bronx Park," 2.4 miles (3.9 km) long; "houses the borough's largest and most diverse shopping district" and geographically separates the North Bronx from the South Bronx. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_Road
- Managed by "the Fordham Road Business Improvement District, a non-profit organization under contract with the City of New York," which "maintains, develops, and markets the merchants along Fordham Road and hosts many community events." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fordham_Road
