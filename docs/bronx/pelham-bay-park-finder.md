## Neighborhood-level history and orientation

- Pelham Bay Park is 2,772 acres (1,122 ha), "the largest public park in New York City," more than three times the size of Manhattan's Central Park, with 13 miles (21 km) of shoreline. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park
- The Siwanoy, whose name means "southern people," were "the first Native American tribe to inhabit the Long Island Sound's northern shoreline" and used the site as a ceremonial and burial ground, evidenced by wampum belts found in the area. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park
- The Siwanoy's territory extended "from Hell Gate to Norwalk, Connecticut, and as far inland as White Plains" by 1640, with settlements including Poningo (near modern Rye), a stockade at Ann Hook's Neck (Rodman's Neck), and a stockade/spiritual center on Hunter Island. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siwanoy
- On June 27, 1654, five Siwanoy sagamores deeded roughly 9,160 acres east of the Hutchinson River northward to Mamaroneck to Thomas Pell — encompassing modern Pelham, New Rochelle, the Pelham Islands, and portions of the Bronx — in a treaty signed under the "Treaty Oak" near the site of the present Bartow-Pell Mansion; Wampage I (also called Anhōōke) was among the signers. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siwanoy
- Separately, Pelham Bay Park's own article states Pell "purchased 50,000 acres (20,000 ha) from the Siwanoy" in 1654, and that the land became known as Pelham Manor after Charles II's 1666 charter. **Acreage discrepancy, not resolved here**: 9,160 acres per the Siwanoy article versus 50,000 acres per the Pelham Bay Park article. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siwanoy
- Pell died in 1669, willing the property to his nephew John; on John Pell's death in 1700 it passed to his son Joseph, then to Joseph's son John, and ownership then passed to the Bartow family, maternal descendants of the Pells. John Pell sold off City Island in 1685. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park
- The city acquired the land in 1887 and it officially became a park in 1888; the Bronx Parks Department paid $2,746,688 (about $98,422,987 in 2025 dollars), assembling more than 1,700 acres from multiple estates. Ownership passed to New York City in 1895 when the Bronx east of the Bronx River was annexed. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park
- The park is operated by the New York City Department of Parks and Recreation. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park

### Islands and peninsulas

- **Hunter Island** is a 166-acre peninsula (previously 215 acres before 1930s landfill) on Long Island Sound's northwestern shore; the Siwanoy called it "Laap-Ha-Wach King," meaning "place of stringing beads." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_Island_(Bronx)
- Joshua Pell took ownership of the site in 1743 as part of the larger Pell estate; John Hunter purchased the property in 1804, moved his family there in 1811/1813 (two sources give different years — see flag below), and built an English Georgian-style mansion at the island's highest point, 90 feet above sea level, described as having "three stories, a large veranda, and terraced gardens" and an art collection of over 400 works including Rembrandt and Leonardo da Vinci. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_Island_(Bronx) and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park
- **Date flag, not resolved here:** Hunter Island's own article gives 1811 for the mansion and says the Hunter family occupied the island until 1865; Pelham Bay Park's article gives 1813 as the year the family moved in. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_Island_(Bronx) and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park
- New York City acquired the land in 1889; by 1917 Hunter Island drew half a million seasonal visitors to its campsites. The mansion was demolished in 1937 during Robert Moses's construction of Orchard Beach, which filled in the LeRoy's Bay lagoon to connect Hunter Island to the mainland. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_Island_(Bronx)
- Hunter Island is today part of the Hunter Island Marine Zoology and Geology Sanctuary (established 1967) and carries the Kazimiroff Nature Trail, named for historian Theodore Kazimiroff, opened in 1986. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_Island_(Bronx)
- **Twin Island** — the East and West Twin Islands — "were once true islands in Pelham Bay but are now connected to each other and to Orchard Beach and nearby Rodman's Neck by a landfill created in 1937." Twin Island was restored in 1995 under the Twin Islands Salt Marsh Restoration Project, at a cost of $850,000. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park
- **Two Trees Island** is connected to East Twin Island by a mudflat and "consists of a rocky plateau upon which one can see Orchard Beach and the environmental center." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park
- The Hunter Island Marine Zoology and Geology Sanctuary is located north of Orchard Beach and "encompasses all of Twin Islands, Cat Briar Island, Two Trees Island, and the northeastern shoreline of Hunter Island," containing "the largest continuous oak forest in Pelham Bay Park." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park
- **Rodman's Neck** is a peninsula in the park's central section that "juts out into Long Island Sound"; it was originally called "Anne's Hoeck" or Ann Hook's Neck and was renamed for Samuel Rodman, and it was included in Thomas Pell's 1654 purchase. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodman%27s_Neck
- Rodman operated a ferry "that ran between Minneford Island (now known as City Island)" and Anne's Hoeck, at the location where a modern bridge now stands. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodman%27s_Neck
- The southern third of Rodman's Neck (54 acres) is a NYPD firing range built in 1959, used by multiple agencies including the FBI, Fire Department Fire Marshals, the Correction Department, and Immigration and Customs Enforcement, with seven rifle ranges and 230 total firing points; the remaining wooded section is parkland within Pelham Bay Park. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodman%27s_Neck and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park
- Rodman's Neck's northern side connects to the former Hunter Island and Twin Island, "to form Orchard Beach and a parking lot." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodman%27s_Neck

### The Revolutionary War: Battle of Pell's Point and Glover's Rock

- The Battle of Pell's Point was fought Friday, October 18, 1776, in the Manor of Pelham, now part of the Bronx and Westchester County. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Pell%27s_Point
- 750 American troops under Colonel John Glover deployed behind stone walls along the road from the landing point, falling back through a series of prepared positions as the British advanced, delaying the British and preventing the encirclement of Washington's main army, which was thereby able to withdraw to White Plains. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Pell%27s_Point
- American casualties: 8 killed, 13 wounded. British: 3 killed, 20 wounded per the official report; Hessian casualty estimates are disputed, ranging from 200 to 1,000. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Pell%27s_Point
- **Commander discrepancy, not resolved here:** the dedicated Battle of Pell's Point article states the landing force of 4,000 was British/Hessian troops under General Henry Clinton (with William Howe as overall commander-in-chief), while Pelham Bay Park's own article states "he [General Howe] landed 4,000 men at Pelham." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Pell%27s_Point and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park
- **Glover's Rock**, a large granite glacial erratic in the park, carries a bronze plaque commemorating the battle and Glover's defense as having "save[d] Washington's troops from destruction." Pelham Bay Park's own article states plainly that "contrary to popular belief, the rock had nothing to do with the battle," and that the claim Glover stood on it "is erroneous, as these distances were computed based on an inaccurate map." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Pell%27s_Point
- No dedicated Wikipedia article for Glover's Rock exists; `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glover%27s_Rock` was not attempted this pass given the material already found on the main park page — see Dead ends for related 404s.

### Split Rock and Anne Hutchinson

- Split Rock is described as "a large dome-shaped granite boulder measuring approximately 25 feet (7.6 m) from north to south and 15 feet (4.6 m) from east to west." — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park
- A tablet inscription associated with the site states "In 1643 She and her Household were Massacred by Indians," referring to Anne Hutchinson. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park
- The New York State Legislature approved placing a bronze tablet on Split Rock honoring Hutchinson in 1904; the Colonial Dames of New York installed it in 1911; it was stolen in 1914. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park
- Split Rock and oak-hickory forest border the Split Rock Golf Course and fall within the Thomas Pell Wildlife Sanctuary (see below). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park

### Thomas Pell Wildlife Sanctuary, the lagoon, and salt marsh

- The Thomas Pell Wildlife Sanctuary, named for Thomas Pell, makes up "the westerly part of Pelham Bay Park," including Goose Creek Marsh (once part of a 5,000-acre wetland), saltwater wetlands adjoining the Hutchinson River, Goose Island, Split Rock, and oak-hickory forest bordering the Split Rock Golf Course. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Pell_Wildlife_Sanctuary
- The Thomas Pell Wildlife Sanctuary and the Hunter Island Marine Zoology and Geology Sanctuary together total 489 acres (1.98 km²); both were created in 1967 in response to opposition to a planned landfill. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Pell_Wildlife_Sanctuary
- Forest within the sanctuary is estimated at "at least three centuries old, dating to colonial times"; wildlife includes raccoon, egrets, hawks, and coyotes. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Pell_Wildlife_Sanctuary and https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park
- The park's lagoon was called "LeRoy's Bay" until the mid-20th century; most of it was filled during the mid-1930s reconstruction of Orchard Beach. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park
- The lagoon was the site of the 1964 Summer Olympics rowing trials, for which it was widened and dredged into a four-lane, 2,000-meter (6,600 ft) rowing course. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park
- The park contains 195 acres (79 ha) of salt marsh, including marsh bordering the Split Rock Golf Course. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelham_Bay_Park

### 2. Orchard Beach
